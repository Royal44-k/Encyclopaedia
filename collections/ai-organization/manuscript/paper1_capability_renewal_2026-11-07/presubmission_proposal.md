# Task routing and expertise renewal in human AI teams

Perspective proposal for author review. Local draft only; not submitted.

## Abstract

Generative AI changes not only how work is completed but also who encounters the problems from which expertise develops. This Perspective examines how diagnostic work, repair attempts and feedback are distributed within teams containing members with different experience. Building on research on automation, workplace learning and expertise renewal, we propose a measurement and testing framework centred on capability coverage: which members can detect, diagnose, repair or appropriately escalate which classes of error, under specified tool conditions. Its central comparison concerns whether teams with comparable resources can develop different distributions of capability that matter under particular member absences or changes in task requirements. We distinguish interventions that combine participation with additional support from tests of routing itself, and individual capability from collective recovery. AI-supported rehearsal, effective specialization and reliable external assistance provide competing pathways rather than exceptions to a decline narrative. Existing evidence motivates these comparisons but does not directly establish the proposed team-level relationships. The framework connects learning opportunities to explicit future responsibilities without presuming that every member must retain every skill.

## Rationale and contribution

The manuscript connects workplace learning, team cross-training and human-AI work design through a coordinated measurement and testing agenda. It asks who encounters diagnostic problems, what each member subsequently learns to do, and how the team recovers under specified changes in personnel or tasks. It builds explicitly on automation research, workplace participation and recent expertise-renewal models, including their treatments of task allocation, substitute practice and multidimensional capability.

The central test compares routing arrangements under constrained practice and support budgets, crossed with independently assigned member availability. The directional prediction is that broader exposure to targeted error classes yields a larger relative recovery benefit when a prespecified baseline specialist is unavailable than when the full team is present. Individual capability and collective recovery are measured separately; mediation is not inferred from their joint change. The framework accommodates effective specialization, beneficial AI-supported rehearsal and reliable external assistance. This connects learning and organizational design to empirically discriminable deployment choices, without claiming an AI-specific psychological mechanism or new empirical findings.
