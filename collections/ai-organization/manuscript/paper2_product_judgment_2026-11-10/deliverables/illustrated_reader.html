    观点论文

    从快速试做到判断积累

    AI辅助产品探索中的科学学习与跨项目迁移

    Richard T.Millian

    Hangzhou Normal University

    摘要

    生成式人工智能使小团队更容易制作产品原型，却没有消除对客户需求和持续投入价值的不确定性。一次试用失败可以引出需求不足、客户错配或使用障碍等不同解释；增加试做次数也未必使团队在下一项目中判断得更准确。本文结合科学创业实验、市场机会识别、类比迁移与AI辅助决策研究，提出一个以跨项目学习为中心的概念框架。框架区分制作、证据获取、项目取舍与经验迁移，并将经验表述为带有客户条件、替代解释和适用边界的判断。我们据此讨论验证顺序如何随关键不确定性变化，提出可检验的命题及训练设计。评价重点是新项目中的客户行为预测、证据选择和资源取舍，同时考虑无效延续与过早终止。该框架尚待行为研究检验，适用于把快速试做转化为累积学习的研究与实践。

    产品完成以后

    一款产品已经可以演示，团队却还无法回答下一步该不该继续投入。设想一个为本地商家开发营销工具的小团队：AI能够生成整洁的宣传海报，试用者表示喜欢，付费和重复使用却迟迟没有出现。团队可以继续改善版式，也可以寻找另一群客户，或者结束项目。每种行动背后，都有一个对失败原因的判断。这个说明情境中的困难，在于同一结果能够支持多种解释；更漂亮的原型不会自动排除其中任何一种。

    腾讯研究院关于超级个体与超级团队的报告，以快速开发、跨职能实践和行动中学习描绘组织变化。其中，flomo的实践把部分需求验证前移：产品人员借助AI跑通原型，再交由工程师上线；其他案例也描述了设计师与业务人员参与开发的变化。报告在结尾追问能力增强之后应当选择什么值得做。1 这些观察把研究视线引向产品探索中的取舍，但报告中的案例并未识别AI对市场判断的因果影响。对小团队而言，一个更具体的问题是：试做更容易之后，每次尝试能否留下足以改善下一次决定的知识？

    已有研究为这一问题提供了基础。科学创业研究将价值解释、假设和实验连接起来；AI辅助创业研究则开始考察对话如何支持商业模式学习。2, 3 本文沿着这些工作进一步讨论跨项目迁移。我们提出，团队需要保留经验成立的条件，在新项目中重新识别这些条件，并据此形成可被后续行为检验的预测。本文是基于定向文献整合的观点论文，提出机制与研究设计，不报告新的实验结果。

    制作速度与证据质量

    原型制作、证据获取和项目选择可以以不同速度改善。可运行的软件、可观看的演示和可使用的服务，分别使某些问题更容易被检验，但它们本身并不等于客户证据。团队需要知道原型正在检验什么：技术能否运行，客户能否完成任务，还是客户是否愿意为结果改变既有行为。若把这些问题合并为一个笼统的“验证成功”，后续投入就缺少清楚依据。

    数字实验研究已经把实验与识别、扩展和放弃商业机会联系起来。Koning等利用创业企业采用A/B测试技术的时间差异研究这一关系；其证据支持把实验看作组织学习的一部分，但不能直接外推为AI使用的效果。4 AI也可能降低访谈材料整理、测试设计和数据分析的成本。因此，值得检验的是制作与现实验证的相对成本，以及团队如何重新配置时间，而非预先认定工具只会加快制作。

    这里有两条竞争路径。第一条路径中，团队把节省的开发时间用于接触目标客户，设计更有辨别力的测试，因而更快修正重要假设。第二条路径中，团队不断增加功能和演示，将产品完成度当作投入依据，关键市场假设却保持不变。两条路径都可能产生更多原型，只有过程与后续决策记录能够将它们区分。

    AI建议的实际作用也取决于使用情境。Otis等在肯尼亚创业者中随机提供AI商业助手，没有检出显著的总体平均经营改善，但发现基线表现不同的群体存在效应差异。5 这一研究提醒我们把工具访问与建议选择分开；它没有直接测量跨项目产品判断力。本文使用“产品判断力”作为工作定义，指团队在证据有限时界定客户、解释价值、选择验证方式并决定投入的能力，不将其视为已经验证的单一量表。

    把客户定位落实到行为

    一项技术可能服务多个市场，先比较候选机会有助于明确选择依据。Gruber等关于技术创业的研究发现，创业经验、进入市场前识别的机会集合与后续表现之间存在联系，但机会数量增加带来的收益会递减。6 对AI辅助探索而言，扩展候选市场之后还需要判断哪些差异真正影响采用和购买。

    以营销工具为例，偶尔做活动的单店店主，与需要协调多家门店的品牌运营人员，虽然都属于餐饮行业，面对的任务频率、责任和购买流程却可能不同。客户定位应记录使用情境、现有替代、使用者、付款者及切换成本。人口统计特征可以辅助描述，但不能取代对任务和决策过程的理解。所谓精细划分，应提高对新客户行为的解释和预测能力。

    细分规则也可能被小样本误导。团队反复切分少量试用者，总能找到某个表现较好的群体。因此，探索阶段形成的规则应在新的客户样本中验证，并保留招募渠道及未参与者的信息。访谈中的兴趣、实际试用、重复使用和付款支持不同判断；对低频任务，观察期必须覆盖真实使用机会。客户价值、企业可持续供给和短期收入也需要分别考察。

    模拟客户不能自动填补现实证据的缺口。Brand等的工作论文比较模型与人类的支付意愿，发现模型估计存在偏差，利用既有调查数据调整模型的收益也受到产品类别和人群边界限制。7 AI可以帮助形成备选解释与访谈问题，但同一个系统生成的客户画像、需求回答和商业论证，不能被当作彼此独立的市场确认。

    失败结果需要被诊断

    在前述情境中，低使用可能意味着客户很少需要制作海报，也可能意味着客户需要这项服务，却无法顺利完成配置。前者要求重新评估目标客户或价值主张，后者可能值得投入实施支持。若没有观察客户的任务过程，两种解释都可能被写进一篇流畅的复盘，却没有获得更强证据。

    我们建议围绕关键假设组织诊断。需求假设涉及问题是否频繁而重要；采用假设涉及客户能否将方案纳入工作；商业模式假设涉及付款者、价格和服务成本。实施失败与证据不足应单独保留：一个未能可靠运行的原型，无法公平检验完整的价值主张；一项没有覆盖客户使用机会的测试，也不足以否定需求。

    对失败的回顾还可能受到事后解释影响。Boso等对创业者的纵向观察将失败经历、学习与后续企业表现联系起来，但这种设计不足以保证每次失败都产生学习，或完整识别其中的因果过程。8 为降低解释随结果漂移的问题，团队可在测试前记录预测、样本来源、观察期、替代解释和取舍条件，失败后再逐项更新。若证据无法区分解释，应留下“尚不能判断”，而非强行提炼一条教训。

    及时终止无效项目是选择质量的一部分，过早放弃同样有成本。Camuffo等的四项随机试验表明，科学决策训练能够影响项目终止和转向模式，但终止次数本身不应成为训练目标。2 决定继续时，需要考虑下一次验证是否可能改变选择及其成本；决定停止时，则需要检查否定性证据是否真正针对核心假设。

    验证顺序取决于待解问题

    先访谈还是先制作原型，取决于当前需要获得哪种证据。若连任务是否经常发生、购买者是谁都不清楚，直接接触客户可能更有价值。若客户只有在体验之后才能识别某项交互需求，最小原型则可能成为必要的研究工具。若产品需要嵌入复杂工作流程，持续参与能够帮助双方共同形成问题和方案。

    客户参与也有成本。Chang和Taylor的元分析报告了客户参与效果随产品开发阶段与情境变化的模式，提示持续参与不应被当作无条件有效的原则。9 这些汇总关系并不构成AI时代探索顺序的随机比较。共创过程中还需观察积极客户是否具有代表性，以及围绕他们形成的方案能否被新的客户使用。

    Agarwal等在坦桑尼亚农业创业者中比较两种训练路径，发现将价值形成逻辑与证据检验结合的条件具有经营表现优势。10 这为围绕因果解释组织测试提供依据，也保留了情境边界。一个原型的作用应由它能够区分的解释来评价。低成本的人工服务、任务观察或购买流程访谈，有时足以检验某个关键环节，无需等待完整系统。

    经验在下一项目中接受检验

    一条可迁移的经验需要同时说明关系与条件。营销工具的失败或许支持这样的暂时判断：当任务低频且免费替代充分时，单纯改善输出质量未必带来持续付费。它没有理由扩展为“所有小商家都不值得服务”。同样，新项目即使服务另一行业，只要面对相似的任务频率和替代条件，旧经验也可能值得检验。

    类比迁移研究提供了一条训练线索。Gentner等在谈判任务中发现，比较案例之间的关系结构有助于形成和使用抽象策略。11 这支持设计跨案例比较，但并不证明同样训练能够改善真实产品投资。迁移要求识别结构相似性，也要求识别决定性差异；只寻找相似之处，可能让过去的失败成为新的偏见。

    据此，复盘产物可包括原有预测、观察证据、被削弱的解释、仍然成立的解释、适用条件，以及下一项目的检验方式。记录只是外部支持。若团队在新项目中仍然无法辨别付款者或选择有效测试，即使复盘写得完整，也不能据此认定判断力提高。反过来，有些团队可能通过讨论形成准确判断，却留下很少文字；研究需要同时观察记录和行为。

    这一分析提出三项可反驳命题。第一，在同等资源下，围绕明确价值解释选择证据，比单纯增加试做，更可能改善项目取舍；若额外制作本身提供了同样有辨别力的反馈，差异可能缩小。第二，带有条件比较和新情境预测的复盘，比一般总结，更可能改善跨项目判断；若训练只改善了当前作品或自信，这一命题不获支持。第三，迁移效应受到条件匹配影响；表面相似而关键条件改变时，机械应用旧经验可能产生负迁移。

    把训练落实为可观察的行为

    训练可以从一对有意安排的案例开始。两种产品在外观和行业上不同，却面临相同的使用者与付款者分离问题；另一对产品表面相似，任务频率与替代方案却不同。参与者先独立选择客户与验证方法，说明理由，再比较证据。比较结束后，要求其在第三个案例中提出预测，使训练从案例理解推进到迁移检验。

    完整训练拟覆盖客户与任务识别、价值解释、验证设计、失败诊断、资源取舍和跨项目迁移。每一环节都应留下可检查的决定，而非仅记录参与时长。例如，验证设计需要说明何种结果会改变下一步行动；失败诊断需要提出能够区分解释的新增证据；资源取舍同时面对值得继续和应当停止的案例。训练内容是本框架的实践推论，其效果尚未得到验证。

    AI可以辅助整理来源材料、提出反例、制作最小原型和检索旧记录。Kostis等已经区分了与AI对话中的不同学习方式，国内研究构想也讨论了AI进入科学决策各环节的作用。3, 12 本文所强调的评价单位是下一项目的行为变化，而非对话流畅度。若训练结束后只有AI保持相同上下文时表现改善，研究应将其描述为系统辅助绩效，并与人的独立迁移区分。

    怎样检验框架是否成立

    一个可行的初步实验可交叉安排AI工具访问与结构化训练，对照组接受同等时长和关注的常规产品实践。参与者先完成项目A，再面对表面不同的项目B；新项目同时包含条件延续与条件改变的案例。训练后的新情境判断是主要评价对象，延迟测量用于区分短期记忆和较稳定的学习。若研究团队决策，应按团队分配并处理群内相关。

    客户行为预测可以用预先规定事件及观察期的概率预测评价校准，项目取舍则需独立的结果基准。评价同时计入错误继续和错误停止，并说明不同错误的后果。商业结果受到市场时点、执行与运气影响，单次收入不能充当判断能力的充分指标。证据选择、预测更新、理由质量和后续客户行为应相互补充，而非由一项自评问卷承担所有解释。

    实验还需公平比较资源和材料。固定并记录模型版本、访问方式及客户证据来源，使用未参与训练的新案例，保留负面和正面结果。不能让同一个模型生成客户真值、协助决策并独自评定成效。机制检验可以进一步拆分“保留记录”“比较条件”和“新项目预测”，确定哪一部分确有贡献。过程变量与结果相关，并不足以证明中介因果链。

    公开材料能够支持先行研究，但也限制推论。连续产品发布、定价变动和公开终止说明可以重建部分决策轨迹；点赞不等于需求，网站仍在不等于留存，发布频率也不能识别AI使用。如果缺少失败前的预测或同一团队的后续项目，分析只能讨论可见的叙事和行为关联，不能声称已经识别学习迁移。正式实证研究还需预注册主要结果与分析规则，并依据最小有意义效应、聚类和失访确定样本量。

    判断积累的边界

    本框架强调在尝试之间保存可修正的关系解释。它承接科学创业与类比学习研究，将评价重心放到新项目中的行为。当前证据不能确定训练的最佳周期、收益持续多久，或何种团队最适合使用。客户需求也可能在产品使用和社会互动中形成，早期证据有限并不必然意味着机会不存在。

    因此，敏锐的产品判断需要容纳不确定性。它体现在知道哪项假设最值得先检验、哪些证据能够改变决定，以及旧经验何时需要停止适用。一个结束的项目仍可能留下有价值的知识；一个继续增长的项目也可能掩盖错误理解。AI辅助探索值得追求的累积成果，是团队能够在下一次投入之前提出更清楚的问题，并在现实证据到来时作出更有根据的修正。

    PERSPECTIVE

    Building product judgment through AI-assisted experimentation

    Scientific learning and transfer across product projects

    Richard T.Millian

    Hangzhou Normal University

    Abstract

    Generative artificial intelligence makes product prototypes easier to build, but uncertainty about customer demand and further investment remains. A disappointing trial can reflect weak demand, a poorly chosen customer segment or barriers to adoption. Repeating such trials need not improve decisions in the next project. Drawing on scientific entrepreneurship, market opportunity identification, analogical transfer and AI-assisted decision research, this Perspective develops a framework for learning across product projects. It distinguishes production, evidence acquisition, project selection and transfer, and represents experience as provisional judgments with explicit conditions and competing explanations. The framework relates validation sequences to the uncertainty being investigated and develops testable propositions and a training design. Evaluation centres on customer-behaviour predictions, evidence choices and resource allocation in new projects, considering both unproductive continuation and premature termination. The framework requires behavioural validation; it provides a basis for investigating how rapid prototyping can support cumulative learning.

    After the product is built

    A product is ready to demonstrate, yet its team cannot decide whether to invest further. Consider a hypothetical team building a marketing tool for local merchants. AI produces attractive promotional posters. Trial users express interest, but payment and repeated use remain limited. The team could improve the layouts, approach another customer segment or end the project. Each action depends on an interpretation of the disappointing response. The difficulty is that the same observation admits several explanations. A more polished prototype does not automatically distinguish them.

    The Tencent Research Institute report on AI-enabled individuals and teams describes rapid development, work across occupational boundaries and learning through action. In its account of flomo, product staff use AI to test working prototypes before handing them to engineers for release. Other cases describe designers and business staff taking on development work. The report also asks how increased capability should inform the choice of worthwhile work.1 These observations motivate an inquiry into product selection, but the report's cases do not identify the causal effect of AI on market judgment. For a small team, the practical question is whether each attempt leaves knowledge that can improve its next decision.

    Scientific entrepreneurship connects theories of value, hypotheses and experiments. Research on AI-assisted entrepreneurship has also begun to examine learning through conversations about business models.2, 3 Building on these approaches, this Perspective focuses on transfer across projects. We propose that teams retain the conditions under which an inference holds, identify those conditions in a new project and make predictions that subsequent behaviour can test. This is a conceptual synthesis based on a targeted literature search. It develops a mechanism and research design without reporting new experimental findings.

    Production speed and evidence quality

    Prototype production, evidence acquisition and project selection can improve at different rates. Working software, a demonstration and a usable service each make certain questions easier to investigate, but none constitutes customer evidence on its own. A team needs to specify what a prototype tests: technical feasibility, successful task completion or a customer's willingness to change existing behaviour. Treating these questions as a single validation outcome leaves subsequent investment poorly grounded.

    Research on digital experimentation already connects testing to the identification, expansion and abandonment of business opportunities. Koning and colleagues examine this relationship using variation in when start-ups adopted A/B testing. Their evidence supports treating experimentation as part of organizational learning, but does not establish an effect of AI adoption.4 AI may also reduce the costs of organizing interviews, designing tests and analysing data. The relevant question concerns relative costs and the reallocation of effort, rather than an assumption that the technology only accelerates production.

    Two pathways follow. Teams might redirect saved development time towards relevant customers and tests that distinguish competing explanations. Alternatively, they might add features and demonstrations while leaving their central market assumptions unchanged. Both pathways can produce more prototypes. Records of evidence acquisition and subsequent decisions are needed to distinguish them.

    The effects of AI advice also depend on context. A field experiment by Otis and colleagues randomized access to an AI business assistant among Kenyan entrepreneurs. It did not detect a significant average improvement in business performance, but identified differences by baseline performance.5 The study motivates separating access from the selection of advice; it does not directly measure product judgment across projects. We use product judgment as a working definition for the capacity to specify customers, explain value, select tests and allocate resources under incomplete evidence. We do not treat it as an already validated unitary scale.

    Grounding customer segments in behaviour

    A technology may serve several markets. Comparing candidate opportunities makes the basis of selection explicit. Gruber and colleagues connect entrepreneurial experience, the set of opportunities identified before market entry and subsequent performance, with diminishing returns to the number of opportunities.6 In AI-assisted exploration, expanding the candidate set still leaves the task of identifying differences that matter for adoption and purchasing.

    For the marketing tool, an owner who occasionally promotes a single restaurant may face different tasks and purchasing arrangements from an operator coordinating several branches. Customer specification should capture the use context, existing alternatives, user, payer and switching costs. Demographic characteristics can supplement this account. Finer segmentation is useful when it improves explanation and prediction for new customers.

    Small samples can generate misleading segments. Repeatedly partitioning a few trial users can reveal a group that appears promising by chance. Segmentation rules developed during exploration therefore need evaluation with fresh customers, alongside records of recruitment channels and non-participation. Expressed interest, trial, repeated use and payment support different inferences. For infrequent tasks, the observation window must include realistic opportunities to use the product. Customer benefit, sustainable provision and short-term revenue also require separate consideration.

    Simulated customers do not automatically resolve missing field evidence. In a working paper, Brand and colleagues find discrepancies between model-derived and human willingness-to-pay estimates, with limits to transferring improvements across product categories and customer populations.7 AI can help generate alternative explanations and interview questions. However, personas, customer answers and business arguments produced by one system should not count as independent confirmations of market demand.

    Diagnosing disappointing outcomes

    In the opening scenario, low use may mean that customers rarely need posters. It may instead mean that interested customers cannot complete setup. The first explanation calls for reconsidering the segment or value proposition; the second may justify implementation support. Without observing the task, either explanation can enter a persuasive retrospective account without becoming better supported.

    Diagnosis can be organized around the assumptions at stake. Demand assumptions concern the frequency and importance of a problem. Adoption assumptions concern whether customers can incorporate a solution into their work. Business-model assumptions concern the payer, price and cost of provision. Implementation failure and insufficient evidence should remain separate possibilities. An unreliable prototype cannot fairly test the full value proposition, and a trial that misses relevant occasions of use cannot adequately test demand.

    Retrospective explanations can also shift with the outcome. Boso and colleagues relate prior failure, learning and subsequent venture performance in longitudinal observational research. That design does not establish that every failure produces learning or fully identify the causal process.8 Recording predictions, sampling, observation windows, alternatives and decision conditions before a test makes later revision more inspectable. Where evidence cannot distinguish explanations, the appropriate record is unresolved uncertainty.

    Ending an unpromising project is one part of selection quality; abandoning a valuable opportunity too early also carries a cost. Four randomized trials reported by Camuffo and colleagues show that scientific decision-making training affects termination and pivoting patterns, but termination counts should not become a training objective.2 Continued investment should consider whether another test could change the decision and what obtaining that evidence would cost. Stopping requires checking whether negative evidence actually addresses a central assumption.

    Choosing a validation sequence

    The value of interviewing before prototyping depends on the evidence needed. Direct customer contact may be useful when task frequency or purchasing responsibility is unclear. A minimal prototype may be necessary when customers can recognize an interaction need only after experiencing it. Continuing participation may help both parties develop a problem and solution within a complex workflow.

    Participation also has costs. Chang and Taylor's meta-analysis reports that its relationship with product outcomes varies across development stages and contexts, qualifying the idea that more involvement is invariably beneficial.9 These aggregate relationships do not constitute a randomized comparison of exploration sequences under AI. Teams also need to examine whether active collaborators represent the intended market and whether new customers can use the resulting product.

    In a randomized training comparison among Tanzanian agribusiness entrepreneurs, Agarwal and colleagues find an economic-performance advantage for an approach combining a theory of value with evidence gathering.10 This supports organizing tests around causal reasoning within the limits of that setting. A prototype should be evaluated by the explanations it can distinguish. A manually delivered service, task observation or purchasing-process interview may sometimes test a critical assumption before a complete system is built.

    Testing experience in the next project

    An experience that can travel needs a relationship and its conditions. The marketing-tool example might support a provisional inference: when tasks are infrequent and adequate free alternatives exist, better output quality may not sustain payment. It would not justify concluding that all small merchants are unattractive customers. A later project in another industry could still warrant testing that inference if the relevant conditions recur.

    Analogical-transfer research suggests a training approach. Gentner and colleagues find that comparing relational structures across negotiation cases supports the abstraction and transfer of strategies.11 This motivates cross-case comparison, but does not establish an effect on real product investments. Transfer requires detecting consequential differences as well as similarities. Searching only for resemblance can turn past experience into a source of error.

    A learning record can retain the original prediction, observations, weakened and surviving explanations, applicable conditions and a test in the next project. Such records are external supports. If a team still cannot identify a payer or choose an informative test in a new project, a thorough retrospective document does not establish improved judgment. Conversely, a team may learn through discussion while leaving little text. Research needs to observe both records and behaviour.

    Three falsifiable propositions follow. First, under comparable resources, evidence choices guided by an explicit account of value should improve selection more than simply increasing prototyping. The difference may narrow when additional prototypes provide equally diagnostic feedback. Second, reflection that compares conditions and makes predictions for new contexts should improve transfer more than a general summary. Improvement confined to the current artefact or to confidence would not support this proposition. Third, transfer depends on condition matching. Mechanically applying experience when surface features persist but decisive conditions change may produce negative transfer.

    Training observable decisions

    Training can begin with deliberately paired cases. Two products in different industries may share a separation between users and payers; two superficially similar products may differ in task frequency and available alternatives. Participants first select customers and tests independently and explain their choices. After comparing evidence, they make predictions for a third case, moving assessment beyond understanding the training examples.

    A proposed programme would cover customer and task identification, value explanation, test design, failure diagnosis, resource allocation and transfer. Each component should yield an inspectable decision. Test design requires specifying which result would change the next action. Diagnosis requires evidence that separates explanations. Resource allocation includes both opportunities worth pursuing and projects worth stopping. These components are practical implications of the framework, not a validated curriculum.

    AI can help organize source material, suggest counterexamples, build minimal prototypes and retrieve earlier records. Kostis and colleagues already distinguish forms of learning through conversations with AI, while a Chinese research proposal examines AI throughout scientific entrepreneurial decision making.3, 12 Our evaluation focus is behavioural change in a subsequent project. If improvement occurs only while the AI retains the original context, it should be described as assisted system performance and distinguished from independent human transfer.

    Evaluating the framework

    An initial experiment could cross AI access with structured training, providing the comparison group with equal instructional time and attention. Participants would complete project A and then a superficially different project B. New cases would include both stable and changed underlying conditions. Judgment in these new cases would be the primary target; delayed assessment would help separate short-term recall from more durable learning. Research on team decisions should randomize at team level and account for within-team dependence.

    Customer-behaviour forecasts can assess calibration using prespecified events and observation windows. Project selection requires an independent outcome benchmark. Evaluation should count both mistaken continuation and mistaken termination and specify the consequences of each. Commercial outcomes depend on market timing, execution and chance, so a single revenue observation is not a sufficient measure of judgment. Evidence selection, belief updating, reasons and subsequent customer behaviour should complement one another.

    Fair comparison also requires recording model versions, access conditions and customer-evidence sources, and using fresh cases with positive and negative outcomes. The same model should not generate customer ground truth, assist decisions and serve as the sole evaluator. Component studies could separate record keeping, condition comparison and prediction in new projects. Associations between process measures and outcomes alone would not establish causal mediation.

    Public records can support preliminary inquiry within narrower limits. Successive launches, pricing changes and termination accounts may reconstruct parts of a decision trajectory. Votes do not establish demand, an operating website does not establish retention, and launch frequency does not identify AI use. Without earlier predictions or subsequent projects by the same team, analysis concerns visible narratives and associations rather than identified learning transfer. A formal empirical study would also preregister primary outcomes and analysis rules, with sample size based on meaningful effect sizes, clustering and attrition.

    The limits of cumulative judgment

    The framework emphasizes retaining revisable explanations between attempts. It builds on scientific entrepreneurship and analogical learning while placing evaluation in a new project. Current evidence does not determine an optimal training duration, how long benefits persist or which teams benefit most. Demand can also emerge through use and social interaction; limited early evidence need not imply that an opportunity has no value.

    Product judgment therefore needs to accommodate uncertainty. It includes recognizing which assumption deserves the next test, what evidence could change a decision and when earlier experience no longer applies. An ended project may leave useful knowledge, while a growing project may conceal a mistaken explanation. The cumulative achievement worth investigating is a team's ability to ask more precise questions before its next investment and revise its decisions when relevant evidence arrives.
