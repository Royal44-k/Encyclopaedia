Health economic value and choice in the age of 
artificial intelligence 
Richard T. Millian 
Hangzhou Normal University 
Abstract 
Overtime pay, convenient meals and faster work with artificial intelligence (AI) offer visible gains in 
money or time. Changes in sleep, physical burden and recovery may receive less attention. Drawing on 
health capital, time allocation, health capability and decision analysis, this Perspective proposes a 
framework for comparing health-related consequences. It compares feasible options over a common 
horizon, recording resources and time, health and functioning, experience, and capabilities and agency 
before appraisal against personal priorities. Measurement, prediction and judgement remain distinct; 
outcomes retain their units, timing and uncertainty. Faster tasks need not create more rest, and better 
assisted performance need not improve independent ability. Cases involving overtime, food choices and 
digital leisure illustrate the comparison. Testable propositions concern time allocation, persistence of 
effects and decision quality. The framework clarifies choices and consequences while allowing priorities 
to differ across people and circumstances. 
Whether overtime pay improves daily life depends on both the income gained and what it 
requires. Longer daytime work, repeated night shifts and greater efficiency with artificial 
intelligence (AI) may all increase income, with different implications for sleep, energy and 
family time. Comparison begins with concrete questions: how much money is gained, where 
time goes, and whether the person can work, study or care for family the next day. Fig. 1 5 
separates measurement, prediction and appraisal; Extended Data Fig. 1 brings the comparison 
framework together. 
Established theories offer different starting points. Grossman treats health as a stock with 
both consumption and production value; Becker brings non-market time into resource 
allocation1,2. Marx links the working day to bargaining and the reproduction of labour power 10 
(https://www.marxists.org/archive/marx/works/1867-c1/ch10.htm). These accounts address 
health, time and labour relations from distinct perspectives. Marx motivates questions about who 
receives production gains and who bears health burdens. Identifying a particular health loss still 
requires evidence about exposure and the feasible alternative; it cannot simply be added to 
surplus value as a measured quantity. 15

Health capability concerns both achieved health and the real opportunities people have to 
maintain it and pursue their goals3. A person who needs overtime pay for housing or care may 
have the right to refuse without the means to do so. Health-dependent utility also means that the 
same income increment need not yield the same welfare gain in every health state4. Appraisal 
should therefore consider which difficulties the income relieves and the time and health costs of 20 
obtaining it. 
We use health economic value to describe the significance of health and functioning for 
resource allocation and personal choice, including the value of health itself and its support for 
daily activities. It is distinct from earning capacity, total welfare and human worth. The 
framework specifies feasible alternatives, traces their consequences over time, and makes 25 
evidence, uncertainty and personal priorities explicit. A comparison may yield a preference, 
identify the conditions under which a choice is justified, or leave a disagreement unresolved. 
From measurement to appraisal 
A laboratory report can describe a blood glucose level, but cannot by itself establish whether a 
choice is worthwhile. Measurement describes the current state; intervention research estimates 30 
possible consequences of changing diet; willingness to bear costs in money, time or taste 
depends on personal priorities. Measurement, prediction and appraisal therefore require different 
justifications. Reference ranges do not rank human worth, and average study results do not 
directly specify an individual future. 
Monetary valuation can inform defined policy and resource-allocation questions, but 35 
payment does not establish that lost functioning has been restored. Even when a loss is 
irreversible, people still compare prevention, mitigation and feasible alternatives. Acceptance of 
compensation can reflect a considered trade-off or a lack of other ways to meet essential needs. 
Behavioural welfare analysis therefore examines the conditions under which observed choices 
reveal informed preferences5. 40 
Cost–consequence analysis presents outcomes alongside one another, while multicriteria 
decision analysis (MCDA) makes criteria and weights explicit6 
(https://www.gov.uk/guidance/cost-consequence-analysis-health-economic-studies). Established 
decision guides already elicit options, values and support needs

Standardized health-status instruments can supply comparable information; preference-based 
measures such as EQ-5D can support health-economic evaluation when used with the 50 
appropriate valuation framework (https://euroqol.org/faq/what-is-the-difference-between-the-eq-

autonomy, relationships or personally valued capability. Self-determination theory offers a 
complementary account of autonomy, competence and relatedness, without implying that every 
activity has the same meaning for every person7. Reading, gaming, exercise and companionship 55 
therefore enter as alternatives whose consequences require assessment, rather than predetermined 
categories of virtue or harm. 
A common record should accommodate different priorities. When income, sleep and family 
time conflict, the comparison should show the trade-off and test whether changing priorities 
changes the choice. An overall score is interpretable only when scales, weights, uncertainty and 60 
double counting have been addressed. Outcomes without a defensible conversion can retain their 
own units and be reported separately.

Three levels of AI benefit 
Task efficiency is the most direct outcome. Noy and Zhang found faster writing and better output 
in a randomized study of 453 professionals8. Brynjolfsson and colleagues examined a staggered 
rollout covering 5,172 customer-support agents; their main difference-in-differences estimate 90 
indicated about 15% more issues resolved per hour9. The latter study was quasi-experimental, 
measured throughput and lacked compensation data. These studies support productivity gains, 
but who controls the saved time, and whether income or health improves, require separate 
measurement9. Supplementary Table 2 summarizes study designs, outcomes and scope.

A second outcome is capability. Performance with assistance and performance after 95 
assistance is removed require separate measurement. Bastani and colleagues’ education 
experiment illustrates the need for an unaided assessment of learning10. Evaluation should cover 
immediate performance, independent performance and transfer to unfamiliar tasks; this 
experiment does not establish general cognitive harm from AI. Support itself also has value 
when it makes previously difficult activities accessible, even if unaided ability is unchanged. 100 
A third outcome is how long a benefit lasts. In De Freitas and colleagues’ seven-day Study 3, 
loneliness relief appeared mainly in immediate post-interaction measures; persistent 
improvement before interaction was not demonstrated11. Differential attrition and an unmatched 
control duration limit interpretation. Evaluation should record immediate comfort and follow 
whether it lasts. For short-form content and virtual interaction, relevant outcomes also include 105 
enjoyment, sleep, relationships and the ability to stop. Activity labels or duration alone do not 
establish their value. 
Medical advice also raises the question of how information becomes action. Bean and 
colleagues found that users handling hypothetical medical scenarios did not match the models’ 
standalone performance, with identification compared between control and pooled AI 110 
assistance12. This measures scenario judgement, not clinical disease. Facebook-deactivation 
research likewise examines life beyond platform use, although its findings cannot directly 
establish generative AI effects13. Health-literate AI emphasizes comprehension, agency, 
accountability and proportionality14. Separate measures of task performance, sustained capability 
and daily outcomes help put these requirements into practice. 115 
Choosing measures for the decision 
Measures should follow the decision. A dinner comparison may need price, preparation time and 
satiety; a month of night work also calls for sleep, daytime functioning and recovery measures. 
Establish feasible alternatives and a common horizon, then select measures relevant to baseline 
conditions and the person’s concerns. Fig. 2 distinguishes exposure, state and consequences for 120 
daily life. 
Separate exposures, states and consequences. Sleep opportunity, sitting time, diet and screen 
use describe behaviours or environments. Symptoms, laboratory measures and task performance

describe selected states. Their significance depends on daily functioning, experience and valued 
activities. The International Classification of Functioning, Disability and Health (ICF) helps 125 
situate functioning in context (https://www.who.int/standards/classifications/international-
classification-of-functioning-disability-and-health). Adding sedentary time, a correlated 
biomarker and a downstream disease risk into a single score can count the same pathway 
repeatedly. Candidate causal links should therefore be specified before aggregation or mediation 
analysis. 130 
Glucose, lipids, blood pressure, strength, vision and skin symptoms measure different aspects 
of health; “blood quality” is too vague to replace them. Changes need interpretation against 
baseline, measurement conditions and appropriate ranges. Skin discomfort and appearance can 
affect experience and social life, but complexion, hair or body shape cannot establish internal 
organ status. Relationships between physical and psychological health also require measurement. 135 
Differences in health must not become a ranking of dignity or human worth. 
Experience and functioning should be observed at appropriate times. The World Health 
Organization-Five Well-Being Index (WHO-5) records mental well-being over the preceding 
two weeks; it is not a diagnostic test or an instantaneous response measure15. A brief attention 
task, an activity diary and a symptom report likewise capture different constructs. Selection 140 
should consider reliability, responsiveness, language, accessibility and burden. Repeated 
measurement may distinguish transient discomfort from a sustained trajectory, but collecting 
more data is not automatically better and can increase anxiety or exclusion. 
Distinguish observations, predictions and unknowns. An unmeasured effect is not zero; 
relative effects cannot yield personal absolute risks without applicable baseline risks and models. 145 
Keep personal priorities alongside the evidence, and revisit the comparison when circumstances 
or outcomes change.

Trade-offs in everyday settings 
Case 1  Daily pay and overnight supplements 
Suppose ordinary daily pay is CNY 200, with an additional CNY 400 for working until 02:00 or 
CNY 1,000 for working through the night. Assuming all amounts are net of tax and ordinary pay 
is unchanged, daily income is CNY 200, 600 or 1,200 (Fig. 4a). If CNY 400 and 1,000 instead 165 
denote total daily pay, the increments are CNY 200 and 800. Distinguishing base pay, 
supplements and total income prevents an initial accounting error. 
The schedule must then be specified: when work starts, when the person must rise the next 
day, whether another shift follows, and whether commuting or care duties can change. Finishing 
at 02:00 with a day off is a different option from finishing at 02:00 before an early shift. An end 170 
time alone does not establish sleep duration, and comparison should extend beyond the work 
episode to subsequent functioning and recovery. 
Whether the person gains also depends on what the money enables. Paying rent or care costs 
may relieve an immediate difficulty; fatigue, restricted activities and other burdens still belong in 
the comparison. Conversely, the absence of obvious discomfort does not establish an absence of 175 
effects. Confirmed income changes, functioning that needs observation and unknown longer-
term consequences can be listed separately, allowing the person to identify losses they regard as 
unacceptable. 
Work arrangements may also be negotiable. Fewer consecutive night shifts, time off the next 
day or the ability to stop can be included as further options. Comparison should examine whether 180

they meet income needs while preserving rest and valued activities. Their effects still require

Case 2  Comparing a week with a month 
Consider a hypothetical 28-day window. Option A offers a net supplement of CNY 10,000 for 
seven overtime days; option B offers CNY 100,000 for 28. Holding ordinary earnings equal, ΔY 185 
= 100,000 − 10,000 = 90,000 CNY, alongside 21 additional overtime days. Fig. 4b aligns the 
denominators. It does not assign the remaining days in option A a health value automatically. 
Their activities, recovery opportunities and effects on family members must be recorded; effects 
extending beyond day 28 require a longer outcome horizon. 
Appraisal also needs shift duration and timing, baseline health, the ability to stop, essential 190 
financial needs and the next-best feasible option. Different constraints may lead to different 
choices, while health costs still belong in the record. Whether CNY 100,000 is worthwhile can 
only be judged under these conditions. A legal or contractual payment addresses responsibility 
and compensation; it does not show that lost functioning has been restored. 
Sleep restriction also shows why subjective adaptation and objective function should be 195 
distinguished. In Van Dongen and colleagues' laboratory programme, 35 adults were randomized 
to 4, 6 or 8 hours in bed for 14 nights; a separate total-deprivation experiment involved 13 adults. 
Performance deficits accumulated in the 4- and 6-hour groups, while reported sleepiness did not 
track their full progression19. These functional findings do not establish permanent neuronal 
destruction after one late night. Recovery and persistence require their own evidence. 200 
Case 3  Beyond the price of dinner 
Comparing a convenient purchased dinner with a meal prepared at home requires more than 
ingredients or price. Suppose the alternatives are maintained for four weeks. Food expenditure, 
shopping, cooking and cleaning time, taste and feasibility all enter the comparison (Fig. 4c). A 
person with a kitchen and predictable hours faces different options from a shift worker with 205 
limited cooking facilities. 
Junk food is an everyday label rather than a sufficiently defined research exposure. Food 
composition, processing category, intake and duration need specification: takeaway is not

synonymous with ultra-processed food, nor home cooking with a balanced diet. Hall and 
colleagues observed an energy-intake difference, but no significant between-diet differences in 210 
short-term glucose tolerance or continuous glucose measures. This neither establishes long-term 
equivalence nor supports deterioration in every internal measure17. 
Preparing a meal uses time and labour. Who shops, cooks and cleans should be recorded 
alongside food spending and the time saved by convenience. A practical comparison can begin 
with feasible changes to available meal combinations or preparation burden, then observe the 215 
eating experience and relevant outcomes. Requiring an ideal menu at the outset can overlook the 
person’s circumstances. 
Case 4  Screens, companionship and an evening 
Suppose two hours after dinner are discretionary. Short dramas or games, AI conversation, 
reading, walking and meeting friends are candidate activities (Fig. 4d). Appraisal first asks what 220 
each displaces. Gaming may displace intended sleep for one person and otherwise unstimulating 
time alone for another. Online and offline activities cannot be ranked by their labels alone, and 
leisure need not justify every minute through increased productivity. 
Observation can extend from the evening to the next day and subsequent weeks: was the 
activity enjoyable, did satisfaction persist, could the person stop as planned, and could they carry 225 
out valued activities the next day? Attention needs a specified construct, such as sustained 
attention, switching or memory. Patience likewise needs a defined behaviour; viewing duration 
alone cannot diagnose cognitive decline. These questions organize observation rather than 
establish causal pathways. 
AI companionship should be assessed for both immediate support and sustained change. 230 
Studies have observed loneliness relief after interaction, while follow-up and comparator design 
limit conclusions about persistence11. A personal comparison can track whether use adds to 
existing support, accompanies delayed sleep and can be ended as intended. Enjoyment and 
support have value in themselves; observing subsequent changes can inform decisions about 
future use. 235 
The same principle applies to AI food or medical advice. A credible-sounding answer and an 
appropriate user decision are different outcomes; medical-scenario experiments have

should examine understanding, recognition of applicability and appropriate action, rather than

Testable propositions and evaluation 
Validation has two parts: identifying mechanisms that change health-related outcomes, and 
testing whether the comparison framework improves informed choice. The first supplies 
evidence for the record; the second tests the tool’s usefulness. Extended Data Fig. 3 separates 
these research paths, and Supplementary Table 3 lists propositions and findings that could 245 
weaken them. 
Workplace studies could combine task logs, whole-day diaries and repeated outcomes with 
measures of control over workload and schedules. Randomized policies or credible natural 
experiments would strengthen causal inference. Simple user–non-user comparisons remain 
vulnerable to job selection, workload, prior health and reverse causation. Prespecified 250 
mechanism analyses should avoid obscuring total effects through mechanical adjustment for 
post-treatment time use. 
Tool evaluation should use an active comparator: identical evidence and an established guide, 
with only the intervention group receiving the added record of time, consequences and 
uncertainty. Decision-quality research and the Cochrane review inform comparator and outcome 255 
selection20,21. Prespecify understanding as a potential primary outcome with independent scoring; 
transfer, values–choice concordance and calibrated uncertainty can be secondary outcomes. 
Record completion time, attrition, anxiety, accessibility and withdrawals’ experiences. 
Repeated use may bring learning, fatigue or changed decisions. Preregister the population, target 
effect, sample-size basis, missing-data analysis and multiplicity handling. Interviews can explain 260 
understanding or disagreement, but cannot replace causal estimates. Simplify or discontinue a 
module without meaningful net benefit. Choices closer to the researchers’ preferred lifestyle 
alone do not establish success.

Institutions and distribution 
Work rules, platform design, housing, food access and care responsibilities constrain personal 265 
choices (Extended Data Fig. 4). Income insecurity or workload demands may make rest difficult, 
so an emphasis on self-discipline alone misses the conditions limiting choice. The framework 
can reveal these constraints; expanding feasible options and changing bargaining conditions also 
require organizational and public-policy action. 
The perspective of the comparison must also be explicit. Workers’ net income, firms’ output, 270 
unpaid family care and public spending are separate entries. Compensation is income to a 
recipient but may be a transfer between parties at the societal level. Time saved by one person 
may become another’s checking, correction or care work. Comparing individual, household and 
societal outcomes shows both aggregate change and who bears it. 
Institutions can also use the framework to ask concrete questions: does a productivity policy 275 
return time to workers; does a platform permit meaningful disengagement; and does an 
intervention remain feasible for people with limited money, mobility or digital access? These are 
evaluative questions, not established effects of particular policies. The same information should 
not become a surveillance score used to penalize workers, deny opportunities or blame people 
for constrained choices. Purpose limitation and proportionate collection are part of making the 280 
proposed support usable. 
Conclusions and outlook 
This Perspective brings health-related consequences, their timing and their distribution into one 
comparison. AI use makes these questions pressing because faster task performance need not 
improve independent capability or daily life. A shared record can make such differences explicit 285 
while allowing personal priorities to vary. Whether the framework improves explanation, 
prediction or decision quality beyond existing methods remains an empirical question. 
This conceptual Perspective draws together economic theory, measurement approaches and 
selected empirical studies through narrative synthesis. The proposed framework remains 
unvalidated. Broader measurement may add burden, population effects may not apply to 290 
individuals, and priorities can change with circumstances. Context-specific feasibility studies

gains in understanding and choice persist.
