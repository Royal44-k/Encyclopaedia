## Extended-data figure legends

**Extended Data Fig. 1.** **A framework for comparing health-related consequences.** Feasible alternatives and a common horizon define the comparison. Resources and time, health and functioning, experience, and capabilities and agency remain distinct. Priorities inform appraisal. Connections represent analytical steps, not estimated causal effects.

**Extended Data Fig. 2.** **Task savings and the allocation of a day.** **a,** The accounting identity includes prompting, verification and rework in T1, additional tasks in A and other work-time changes in B. **b,** Hypothetical 24-hour budgets with an 8-hour baseline workday, a 4-hour task saving and B = 0. Non-work time is not assigned a health effect.

**Extended Data Fig. 3.** **Two research questions and their evaluation paths.** Mechanism research explains how outcomes arise; tool evaluation tests the added comparison module. An active comparator gives both groups the same facts and established guide. The proposed design is not a completed trial.

**Extended Data Fig. 4.** **Conditions of choice and distribution of consequences.** Workplace, platform and household conditions shape feasible options. Income, time and care burdens may accrue to different people. Arrows organize evaluation questions rather than depict estimated causal effects.
