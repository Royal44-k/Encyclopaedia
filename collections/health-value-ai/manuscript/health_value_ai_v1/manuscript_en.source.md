# Health economic value and intertemporal choice in the age of artificial intelligence

## Abstract

Generative artificial intelligence changes how people produce, obtain information and spend time. Its value for individuals cannot be inferred from task efficiency or immediate satisfaction alone. We develop a conceptual framework for evaluating health-related trade-offs across work and everyday life, drawing on health capital, time allocation, health capability and decision science. The framework compares feasible alternatives over a common horizon, separates resources, health, experience and capabilities, and records uncertainty without imposing a universal monetary conversion. Its distinctive emphasis is the connection between task savings, control over time and subsequent activities, together with the distinction between assisted performance and independent capability. Examples from overtime compensation, food choices and digital companionship illustrate its boundaries. We propose evaluation against established decision support, using understanding, transfer and burden rather than a prescribed lifestyle as success criteria. This is a research agenda for informed choice, not a validated health valuation instrument.

Keywords: health economics; artificial intelligence; health capability; time allocation; intertemporal choice; decision support

## From market rewards to personal welfare

Consider a person offered additional pay for working late. The payment is visible, whereas the consequences for sleep, relationships and subsequent functioning require a different assessment. A larger payment can relieve material constraints while the work imposes burdens. Neither its size nor the presence of a health risk determines the person's overall welfare without information about alternatives, preferences and circumstances. AI-assisted work makes this comparison more complex: completing a task faster may change earnings, workload, discretionary time or the kinds of activity that remain. The relevant question is how these changes enter a person's life over time.

Economic theory already provides substantial foundations for this question. Grossman's health capital model treats health as both consumption and investment, while Becker's time allocation framework incorporates time alongside market goods.[@E001][@E002] The present argument therefore does not rest on a supposed absence of health or non-market activity from economics. Marx's discussion of the working day provides a complementary political-economic motivation: the reproduction of labour-power and the physical and social limits of work make control over time and the distribution of burdens central questions.[@E003] We use that motivation without treating a measured health decrement as a unit of surplus value.

A second foundation concerns what individuals are able to do. Ruger's health capability approach combines health functioning with health agency and explicitly considers personal and contextual conditions; it already proposes a multidimensional profile.[@E004] Health-state-dependent utility research also examines how health can alter the value derived from consumption.[@E005] These contributions rule out a claim that multidimensional assessment, or the interaction of money and health, is new. The narrower contribution proposed here is a procedure connecting AI-related changes in tasks and information to feasible options, time allocation and consequences, with an explicit test of whether this procedure improves decisions.

## Valuation without a universal price

We define health economic value as the contribution of health states and their changes to a person's experiences, feasible activities and welfare over a specified horizon, evaluated relative to an alternative. This is an organizing definition, not a newly identified physical quantity. Health, welfare and the worth of a person are distinct. Equal human dignity and respect for informed choice are normative commitments of the framework; neither productivity nor health status measures a person's moral standing.

Measurement, prediction and appraisal must be separated. Measurement describes a state or resource use. Prediction estimates what would occur under an alternative, with uncertainty. Appraisal concerns how those consequences matter to the person. Receiving compensation does not establish biological restoration. Conversely, limited reversibility does not logically prevent risk comparison or preference elicitation. Any monetary valuation would be conditional on information, resources, baseline circumstances and the decision being evaluated. This framework neither requires such a valuation nor prohibits it when its assumptions are justified.

Observed choice alone is also an incomplete basis for deciding which information or options should count in welfare evaluation. Behavioural welfare analysis makes the relation between choice conditions and welfare an explicit theoretical problem.[@E006] Our proposed response is to record whether an option can actually be refused, what the person understands, and which goals the decision serves. Autonomy should not be equated with self-sufficiency: self-determination theory distinguishes a sense of volition from independence.[@E007] Assistance from AI, another person or a clinical service can therefore be compatible with autonomy; its effects require assessment rather than classification by the presence of assistance.

The assessment methods are likewise established. Cost-consequence analysis presents different costs and effects separately, including effects outside health.[@E008] Multicriteria decision analysis can support aggregation, but criterion overlap, preference assumptions and the meaning of weights require attention.[@E009] The Ottawa Personal Decision Guide already helps people consider options, benefits, harms, values and support for health or social decisions.[@E010] Our profile is intended as a candidate extension for AI-related time and information problems. Merely adding columns or asking people what matters to them would not demonstrate an advance.

Preference-based health measurement also provides an established route to aggregation. EQ-5D distinguishes a descriptive health profile, a person's own health rating and an index based on population preferences; the latter can support quality-adjusted life-year calculations.[@E025] These quantities answer different questions. A self-rating should not be substituted for a preference-weighted index, and a predicted QALY change requires defensible health trajectories over time. Our proposal retains such measures where appropriate while recording other consequences separately. It does not claim to replace them or to infer a QALY loss from a single night of lost sleep.

## Why AI changes the comparison

Three distinctions organize the proposed AI application. First, task efficiency differs from control over the resulting time. Noy and Zhang's randomized study of professional writing found improved task productivity with ChatGPT.[@E011] Such evidence establishes a task-level benefit in a defined setting; it does not establish the destination of any saved time or a subsequent health gain. Verification, corrections, learning to use the tool and additional assignments must enter the accounting. The same technical gain could be followed by more rest, more production or a different activity mix, depending on constraints and preferences.

For matched tasks with comparable quality, let T0 denote time without AI and T1 all time with AI, including prompting, checking and rework. Let S = T0 − T1, A denote time for additional tasks, and B other changes in work time within the same calendar window. Then:

ΔW = −S + A + B.                                                     (1)

Equation (1) is an accounting identity, not an estimated behavioural relationship. Under the additional assumption of a fixed total time budget, non-work time changes by −ΔW; its composition remains undetermined. In a hypothetical example, saving 240 minutes while adding 300 minutes of work, with B = 0, increases total work by 60 minutes. This example contains no health coefficient. Establishing a health pathway requires observing what activities change and the relevant outcomes, rather than converting minutes saved directly into health gains.

Second, assisted performance and independent capability may diverge. A classroom experiment comparing different generative AI tutoring arrangements showed that the design of assistance matters for later unaided mathematics performance.[@E012] The outcome concerns learning in a particular educational setting, not general cognitive deterioration. The implication for assessment is to distinguish successful performance with assistance, retained ability without it, and the person's reasons for delegating a task. Delegation can be valuable even when independent mastery is not the objective.

Third, immediate experience and lasting functioning answer different questions. In the longitudinal component of research on AI companions, interaction relieved loneliness immediately, but the reported pattern did not establish persistent improvement before subsequent interactions.[@E013] In medical information tasks, evaluating the user matters as much as evaluating the model: a randomized study found that ordinary information sources outperformed pooled AI-assisted users in identifying relevant conditions in hypothetical scenarios.[@E014] Neither finding supports a general conclusion that AI is beneficial or harmful. Earlier research on Facebook deactivation likewise illustrates that different welfare-relevant outcomes can move in different directions, although that study is not evidence about generative AI.[@E015]

These distinctions motivate a common comparison procedure across work, information use and leisure. They do not imply that all digital activities share an exposure or mechanism. Recommendation feeds, conversational companions, clinical prediction tools and writing assistants should be recorded separately, with their versions, uses and alternatives.

Recent work on health-literate AI already emphasizes comprehension, agency, accountability and proportionality.[@E024] This closely related argument reinforces the need to define a narrower contribution. The proposed profile focuses on the economic comparison of feasible activities over time and on whether an additional comparison module improves decisions. Supporting understanding or invoking agency alone would not distinguish it from existing work.

## A comparative health consequence profile

The proposed profile begins with a feasible decision, not a health score. For an individual i, an option a is compared with an available alternative b over horizon T. The record contains changes in disposable resources, the allocation of time, physical and mental functioning, experience, and relevant capabilities, together with constraints and the provenance of each prediction. Figure 1 summarizes these stages. Table 1 specifies a minimum record rather than an item bank that every study must administer.

The first step is to make the counterfactual complete. A week of overtime followed by three weeks of ordinary activity cannot be compared with four weeks of overtime while ignoring the remaining weeks in the first option. Costs should be incremental and attributable to the option: total income is different from an overtime supplement, and a medical expenditure is different from a health loss. The profile should also identify effects on other people. An individual account and a household or societal account may require different perspectives; they should not be silently merged.

The second step is to distinguish outcomes from their determinants. Sleep schedules, diet and sedentary time can be exposures or intermediate variables; symptoms, functioning and clinical outcomes belong to other parts of the causal account. Counting each stage as an independent welfare loss can duplicate the same consequence. WHO's ICF framework provides a useful distinction between functioning and its environmental context.[@E016] For this application, skin symptoms, vision, muscle function or metabolic measures should be included when they have a defined role in the decision, not because a larger testing panel appears more comprehensive. There is no universally favourable direction for every laboratory value.

The third step is to match measures to their constructs and time scales. For example, WHO-5 measures mental well-being over the preceding two weeks; it is not a measure of total health value or a diagnostic conclusion.[@E017] Independent task performance, perceived autonomy and immediate enjoyment require their own interpretation. A person can value an activity's pleasure without claiming that it improves long-term health. Equally, a short-term functional change should not be described as permanent damage without evidence of persistence and mechanism.

The fourth step is to make uncertainty visible. Each predicted consequence should specify its source population, comparison, outcome, horizon and uncertainty, and distinguish an observed finding from an extrapolation. Unknown is an admissible entry, distinct from zero. Where probabilities cannot be defended, transparent scenarios are preferable to invented precision. Recovery should be described by the outcome actually observed and the time needed to observe it; irreversibility is an empirical property to investigate, not a default label for every burden.

Finally, appraisal belongs to the person within the stated normative and institutional boundaries. Dominance may resolve a comparison only when relevant dimensions are sufficiently complete, directions of preference are defensible and uncertainty does not overturn the ordering. Otherwise, the profile supports an explained trade-off or an explicitly unresolved choice. It does not force complete rankings. If aggregation is requested, the value functions, ranges and preference assumptions must be elicited and tested; weights cannot be justified merely because they sum to one.[@E009] Population policy decisions additionally require a stated distributional perspective rather than extrapolation from one person's willingness to accept a risk.

## Applications and limits of inference

The overtime example shows why a decision can be important without having an immediately computable answer. A WHO/ILO systematic review reported a relative risk of stroke incidence of 1.35 (95% confidence interval 1.13–1.61) for working at least 55 rather than 35–40 hours per week in the selected cohort synthesis.[@E018] The evidence warrants attention to long working hours, but the estimate does not quantify an individual's risk from a particular month of late nights. Applying it to that problem would require a defensible exposure model, baseline risk and appropriate time horizon. Income can be compared under stated assumptions; overall welfare remains conditional on health consequences, alternatives and the person's goals.

Suppose, purely illustratively, that an extra CNY 10,000 is offered for seven days of overtime and CNY 100,000 for 28 days. Assume that these are net supplements, ordinary earnings are otherwise equal, and the first option is followed by 21 days without overtime. Over the shared 28-day window, the second option adds CNY 90,000 and 21 overtime days. The financial increment is calculable under those assumptions; neither its health cost nor its welfare advantage is supplied by the arithmetic. The comparison should record daily exposure, recovery opportunities and later consequences, extending follow-up beyond the payment period when necessary. No amount is labelled adequate compensation without those additional judgements.

Food choices expose a different limitation. A controlled crossover trial found higher energy intake during an ultra-processed diet than an unprocessed diet, while a sleep-extension trial found reduced energy intake relative to control in adults with overweight and habitual short sleep.[@E019][@E020] These studies support attention to interactions among everyday activities. They do not supply household food budgets, preparation costs or a universal ranking of people who choose differently. Free meal provision, available time and actual purchasing conditions affect the relevance of feeding-study results to daily decisions. Table 2 preserves the principal design and interpretation limits.

Digital companionship provides a third application. Immediate relief may matter even if it does not persist after an interaction.[@E013] The relevant alternative could be another form of entertainment, human contact, unwanted isolation or sleep; assuming that everyone is replacing a beneficial outdoor activity prejudges the comparison. A useful record would distinguish what the person gains during the activity, what happens afterwards, and which activities are displaced. Increasing or decreasing screen time is not by itself an adequate success criterion.

Together, these examples show why health-related choices can be economically meaningful without a common currency. They also delimit the proposed contribution: a profile organizes the comparison; it does not generate missing medical knowledge or establish that people have overlooked consequences. That latter claim requires direct evidence of omission or misunderstanding in the target population.

## Testing whether the framework improves decisions

The framework needs two distinct tests. A mechanism study would investigate how access to a specified AI system changes task time, total work, control over time, activity allocation and subsequent outcomes. Randomizing access could identify an assignment effect in that environment; it would not automatically randomize actual use, organizational practice or each mediator. A credible analysis must distinguish the total effect of assignment from additional assumptions required for mediation. Recording task quality and activities outside the software prevents platform telemetry from being mistaken for a complete account of life.

A decision-support study addresses a different question: whether the proposed comparison procedure adds value. We propose an active-control comparison between an established guide with a common factual information pack and the same guide and facts supplemented by a separate module on time allocation, intertemporal consequences and uncertainty. The original Ottawa guide would remain intact.[@E010] This design tests the added package, including its burden. It cannot attribute an effect to each component; a component study would require a separate design.

Decision quality should be distinguished from the process of deciding and from what happens afterwards.[@E021] The proposed primary outcome is independently scored understanding of the common information, including the comparator, horizon and limits of inference. An unfamiliar transfer task would assess whether any benefit extends beyond the presented layout. Confidence calibration, time required, non-completion and follow-up participation are important additional outcomes. A change towards a lower-paying job, less AI use or a preferred diet would not be scored as intrinsically correct. People may rationally revise their priorities after receiving information.

Evidence from treatment and screening decision aids supports the feasibility of improving knowledge, but it does not determine the incremental effect of this module over an active comparator.[@E022] Sample size should therefore follow a justified target difference and a pilot assessment of outcome variability and retention, rather than importing a favourable pooled effect. Outcome collection should invite all randomized participants, including those who stop using the tool. Missing data, multiple outcomes, subgroup analyses and departures from the analysis plan should be addressed prospectively. Initial fixed materials can keep information constant; introducing a generative interface would create an additional intervention requiring separate evaluation.

The framework should be revised if it adds burden without improving understanding, fails to transfer, obscures important trade-offs or systematically excludes people with fewer resources. A positive understanding result would support a decision aid, not establish a new theory of all health value. Conversely, a null tool effect would not show that health consequences are irrelevant to welfare. These distinctions make the programme capable of yielding informative negative findings.

## Structural implications and boundaries

Individual decision support has a limited remit. Recording that someone cannot refuse overtime identifies a constraint; it does not remove it. Studying how employers, platforms, households and workers share gains and burdens requires evidence about those actors and the available institutional alternatives. The Marxian motivation is retained as a question about control and distribution, rather than treated as an empirical conclusion produced by the profile. Making health consequences more visible must not become a way of transferring responsibility for structural conditions to individuals.

This Perspective is based on purposive reading across economic theory, decision support and selected health and AI studies. It is not a systematic review, and the examples do not estimate a common effect. Their populations, interventions and horizons differ. The proposed measures have not been validated together, preferences may change, and longer-term predictions can be weak even when short-term causal evidence is strong. Restricting the record to what can be measured may itself omit valued aspects of life; expanding it indefinitely can make it unusable. Both problems need participatory development and empirical assessment.

Health economic evaluation in the AI era should make the relation between technical gains and lived consequences explicit. The proposed framework standardizes the questions, comparisons and evidence requirements while leaving room for different defensible choices. Its contribution will depend on whether it improves understanding and makes constraints visible at an acceptable burden. Productivity, enjoyment and health can each matter; the scientific task is to establish how they change under feasible alternatives, and the evaluative task is to explain how those changes bear on the lives people seek to lead.

**A proposed workflow for comparative appraisal.** Feasible alternatives and a common horizon define the comparison.
