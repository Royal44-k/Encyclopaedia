The assessment methods are likewise established. Cost-consequence analysis presents different 
costs and effects separately, including effects outside health.[8] Multicriteria decision analysis 
can support aggregation, but criterion overlap, preference assumptions and the meaning of 75 
weights require attention.[9] The Ottawa Personal Decision Guide already helps people consider 
options, benefits, harms, values and support for health or social decisions.[10] Our profile is 
intended as a candidate extension for AI-related time and information problems. Merely adding 
columns or asking people what matters to them would not demonstrate an advance. 
Preference-based health measurement also provides an established route to aggregation. EQ-5D 80 
distinguishes a descriptive health profile, a person's own health rating and an index based on 
population preferences; the latter can support quality-adjusted life-year calculations.[11] These 
quantities answer different questions. A self-rating should not be substituted for a preference-
weighted index, and a predicted QALY change requires defensible health trajectories over time. 
Our proposal retains such measures where appropriate while recording other consequences 85 
separately. It does not claim to replace them or to infer a QALY loss from a single night of lost 
sleep. 
Why AI changes the comparison 
Three distinctions organize the proposed AI application. First, task efficiency differs from 
control over the resulting time. Noy and Zhang's randomized study of professional writing found 90 
improved task productivity with ChatGPT.[12] Such evidence establishes a task-level benefit in 
a defined setting; it does not establish the destination of any saved time or a subsequent health 
gain. Verification, corrections, learning to use the tool and additional assignments must enter the 
accounting. The same technical gain could be followed by more rest, more production or a 
different activity mix, depending on constraints and preferences. 95
