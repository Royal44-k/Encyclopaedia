Decision quality should be distinguished from the process of deciding and from what happens 
afterwards.[23] The proposed primary outcome is independently scored understanding of the 225 
common information, including the comparator, horizon and limits of inference. An unfamiliar 
transfer task would assess whether any benefit extends beyond the presented layout. Confidence 
calibration, time required, non-completion and follow-up participation are important additional 
outcomes. A change towards a lower-paying job, less AI use or a preferred diet would not be 
scored as intrinsically correct. People may rationally revise their priorities after receiving 230 
information. 
Evidence from treatment and screening decision aids supports the feasibility of improving 
knowledge, but it does not determine the incremental effect of this module over an active 
comparator.[24] Sample size should therefore follow a justified target difference and a pilot 
assessment of outcome variability and retention, rather than importing a favourable pooled effect. 235 
Outcome collection should invite all randomized participants, including those who stop using the 
tool. Missing data, multiple outcomes, subgroup analyses and departures from the analysis plan 
should be addressed prospectively. Initial fixed materials can keep information constant; 
introducing a generative interface would create an additional intervention requiring separate 
evaluation. 240 
The framework should be revised if it adds burden without improving understanding, fails to 
transfer, obscures important trade-offs or systematically excludes people with fewer resources. A 
positive understanding result would support a decision aid, not establish a new theory of all 
health value. Conversely, a null tool effect would not show that health consequences are 
irrelevant to welfare. These distinctions make the programme capable of yielding informative 245 
negative findings.
