A second foundation concerns what individuals are able to do. Ruger's health capability 
approach combines health functioning with health agency and explicitly considers personal and 
contextual conditions; it already proposes a multidimensional profile.[4] Health-state-dependent 45 
utility research also examines how health can alter the value derived from consumption.[5] These 
contributions rule out a claim that multidimensional assessment, or the interaction of money and 
health, is new. The narrower contribution proposed here is a procedure connecting AI-related 
changes in tasks and information to feasible options, time allocation and consequences, with an 
explicit test of whether this procedure improves decisions. 50 
Valuation without a universal price 
We define health economic value as the contribution of health states and their changes to a 
person's experiences, feasible activities and welfare over a specified horizon, evaluated relative 
to an alternative. This is an organizing definition, not a newly identified physical quantity. 
Health, welfare and the worth of a person are distinct. Equal human dignity and respect for 55 
informed choice are normative commitments of the framework; neither productivity nor health 
status measures a person's moral standing. 
Measurement, prediction and appraisal must be separated. Measurement describes a state or 
resource use. Prediction estimates what would occur under an alternative, with uncertainty. 
Appraisal concerns how those consequences matter to the person. Receiving compensation does 60 
not establish biological restoration. Conversely, limited reversibility does not logically prevent 
risk comparison or preference elicitation. Any monetary valuation would be conditional on 
information, resources, baseline circumstances and the decision being evaluated. This framework 
neither requires such a valuation nor prohibits it when its assumptions are justified.
