Dominance may resolve a comparison only when relevant dimensions are sufficiently complete, 
directions of preference are defensible and uncertainty does not overturn the ordering. Otherwise, 
the profile supports an explained trade-off or an explicitly unresolved choice. It does not force 
complete rankings. If aggregation is requested, the value functions, ranges and preference 
assumptions must be elicited and tested; weights cannot be justified merely because they sum to 170 
one.[9] Population policy decisions additionally require a stated distributional perspective rather 
than extrapolation from one person's willingness to accept a risk. 
Applications and limits of inference 
The overtime example shows why a decision can be important without having an immediately 
computable answer. A WHO/ILO systematic review reported a relative risk of stroke incidence 175 
of 1.35 (95% confidence interval 1.13–1.61) for working at least 55 rather than 35–40 hours per 
week in the selected cohort synthesis.[20] The evidence warrants attention to long working hours, 
but the estimate does not quantify an individual's risk from a particular month of late nights. 
Applying it to that problem would require a defensible exposure model, baseline risk and 
appropriate time horizon. Income can be compared under stated assumptions; overall welfare 180 
remains conditional on health consequences, alternatives and the person's goals. 
Suppose, purely illustratively, that an extra CNY 10,000 is offered for seven days of overtime 
and CNY 100,000 for 28 days. Assume that these are net supplements, ordinary earnings are 
otherwise equal, and the first option is followed by 21 days without overtime. Over the shared 
28-day window, the second option adds CNY 90,000 and 21 overtime days. The financial 185 
increment is calculable under those assumptions; neither its health cost nor its welfare advantage 
is supplied by the arithmetic. The comparison should record daily exposure, recovery 
opportunities and later consequences, extending follow-up beyond the payment period when 
necessary. No amount is labelled adequate compensation without those additional judgements.
