# Evidence outline for the first full manuscript

Document: health_value_ai_v1. Date: 2026-11-06. Article type: Perspective / conceptual framework. No original participant data or original causal effect estimates. Intended audience: health economics and behavioural science, with medical relevance. Exact journal selection remains unresolved; this is a portable first-submission format, not a claim of compliance with every journal.

## Argument

Task performance and immediate experience are insufficient proxies for personal welfare. AI changes the production of tasks, the allocation of time, and the information through which people evaluate options. A comparative health consequence profile can make these links explicit without imposing a universal price or preferred lifestyle. Its value must be tested against existing decision support.

## Evidence bound structure

1. Opening problem and intellectual foundations. E001 Grossman, E002 Becker, E003 Marx, E004 Ruger, E005 Finkelstein. No claim that economics overlooks health or that the proposed profile invents multidimensional assessment. Grossman and Becker are cited only for their broad conceptual contribution; source depth limitations remain in the source manifest.
2. Welfare, agency and established assessment. E006 Bernheim–Rangel, E007 Ryan–Deci, E008 cost consequence analysis, E009 MCDA, E010 OPDG. Define health, experience, capabilities, resources and agency separately. The manuscript's normative commitments are explicitly proposals.
3. AI mechanisms. E011 Noy–Zhang final published abstract, E012 Bastani final paper, E013 De Freitas final issue metadata, E014 Bean, E015 Allcott. No fusion of preprint and final sample counts; no task score to neural injury inference.
4. Comparison procedure and operational indicators. Proposed definitions; E016 WHO ICF, E017 WHO-5, E009, E021 decision quality. Specify counterfactual, time horizon, feasibility, uncertainty and non-overlap. No unvalidated omnibus health score.
5. Representative cases. E018 Descatha, E019 Hall, E020 Tasali, E013, E014. Group effects are bounded to their populations and horizons. Monetary and time illustrations are explicitly hypothetical.
6. Testable research programme. E010 OPDG, E021 Sepucha, E022 Stacey, with design proposals identified as such. Active comparator; independent outcomes; ascertain outcomes among all randomized participants; burden and transfer; no inference that tool success establishes all AI-health mechanisms.
7. Limits, structural implications and conclusion. Synthesis and prospective implications, not original findings. A decision guide does not substitute for viable employment, social support or institutional change.
