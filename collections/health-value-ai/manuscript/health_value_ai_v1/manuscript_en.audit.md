## Abstract

Generative artificial intelligence changes how people produce, obtain information and spend time. [claim:C007] [evidence:E900]
Its value for individuals cannot be inferred from task efficiency or immediate satisfaction alone. [claim:C008] [evidence:E900]
We develop a conceptual framework for evaluating health-related trade-offs across work and everyday life, drawing on health capital, time allocation, health capability and decision science. [claim:C009] [evidence:E900]
The framework compares feasible alternatives over a common horizon, separates resources, health, experience and capabilities, and records uncertainty without imposing a universal monetary conversion. [claim:C010] [evidence:E900]
Its distinctive emphasis is the connection between task savings, control over time and subsequent activities, together with the distinction between assisted performance and independent capability. [claim:C011] [evidence:E900]
Examples from overtime compensation, food choices and digital companionship illustrate its boundaries. [claim:C012] [evidence:E900]
We propose evaluation against established decision support, using understanding, transfer and burden rather than a prescribed lifestyle as success criteria. [claim:C013] [evidence:E900]
This is a research agenda for informed choice, not a validated health valuation instrument. [claim:C014] [evidence:E900]


Keywords: health economics; artificial intelligence; health capability; time allocation; intertemporal choice; decision support

## From market rewards to personal welfare

Consider a person offered additional pay for working late. [claim:C015] [evidence:E900]
The payment is visible, whereas the consequences for sleep, relationships and subsequent functioning require a different assessment. [claim:C016] [evidence:E900]
A larger payment can relieve material constraints while the work imposes burdens. [claim:C017] [evidence:E900]
Neither its size nor the presence of a health risk determines the person's overall welfare without information about alternatives, preferences and circumstances. [claim:C018] [evidence:E900]
AI-assisted work makes this comparison more complex: completing a task faster may change earnings, workload, discretionary time or the kinds of activity that remain. [claim:C019] [evidence:E900]
The relevant question is how these changes enter a person's life over time. [claim:C020] [evidence:E900]


Economic theory already provides substantial foundations for this question. [claim:C021] [evidence:E001,E002,E003]
Grossman's health capital model treats health as both consumption and investment, while Becker's time allocation framework incorporates time alongside market goods.[@E001][@E002] The present argument therefore does not rest on a supposed absence of health or non-market activity from economics. [claim:C022] [evidence:E001,E002]
Marx's discussion of the working day provides a complementary political-economic motivation: the reproduction of labour-power and the physical and social limits of work make control over time and the distribution of burdens central questions.[@E003] We use that motivation without treating a measured health decrement as a unit of surplus value. [claim:C023] [evidence:E003]


A second foundation concerns what individuals are able to do. [claim:C024] [evidence:E004,E005]
Ruger's health capability approach combines health functioning with health agency and explicitly considers personal and contextual conditions; it already proposes a multidimensional profile.[@E004] Health-state-dependent utility research also examines how health can alter the value derived from consumption.[@E005] These contributions rule out a claim that multidimensional assessment, or the interaction of money and health, is new. [claim:C025] [evidence:E004,E005]
The narrower contribution proposed here is a procedure connecting AI-related changes in tasks and information to feasible options, time allocation and consequences, with an explicit test of whether this procedure improves decisions. [claim:C026] [evidence:E004,E005]


## Valuation without a universal price

We define health economic value as the contribution of health states and their changes to a person's experiences, feasible activities and welfare over a specified horizon, evaluated relative to an alternative. [claim:C027] [evidence:E900]
This is an organizing definition, not a newly identified physical quantity. [claim:C028] [evidence:E900]
Health, welfare and the worth of a person are distinct. [claim:C029] [evidence:E900]
Equal human dignity and respect for informed choice are normative commitments of the framework; neither productivity nor health status measures a person's moral standing. [claim:C030] [evidence:E900]


Measurement, prediction and appraisal must be separated. [claim:C031] [evidence:E900]
Measurement describes a state or resource use. [claim:C032] [evidence:E900]
Prediction estimates what would occur under an alternative, with uncertainty. [claim:C033] [evidence:E900]
Appraisal concerns how those consequences matter to the person. [claim:C034] [evidence:E900]
Receiving compensation does not establish biological restoration. [claim:C035] [evidence:E900]
Conversely, limited reversibility does not logically prevent risk comparison or preference elicitation. [claim:C036] [evidence:E900]
Any monetary valuation would be conditional on information, resources, baseline circumstances and the decision being evaluated. [claim:C037] [evidence:E900]
This framework neither requires such a valuation nor prohibits it when its assumptions are justified. [claim:C038] [evidence:E900]


Observed choice alone is also an incomplete basis for deciding which information or options should count in welfare evaluation. [claim:C039] [evidence:E006,E007]
Behavioural welfare analysis makes the relation between choice conditions and welfare an explicit theoretical problem.[@E006] Our proposed response is to record whether an option can actually be refused, what the person understands, and which goals the decision serves. [claim:C040] [evidence:E006]
Autonomy should not be equated with self-sufficiency: self-determination theory distinguishes a sense of volition from independence.[@E007] Assistance from AI, another person or a clinical service can therefore be compatible with autonomy; its effects require assessment rather than classification by the presence of assistance. [claim:C041] [evidence:E007]


The assessment methods are likewise established. [claim:C042] [evidence:E008,E009,E010]
Cost-consequence analysis presents different costs and effects separately, including effects outside health.[@E008] Multicriteria decision analysis can support aggregation, but criterion overlap, preference assumptions and the meaning of weights require attention.[@E009] The Ottawa Personal Decision Guide already helps people consider options, benefits, harms, values and support for health or social decisions.[@E010] Our profile is intended as a candidate extension for AI-related time and information problems. [claim:C043] [evidence:E008,E009,E010]
Merely adding columns or asking people what matters to them would not demonstrate an advance. [claim:C044] [evidence:E008,E009,E010]


Preference-based health measurement also provides an established route to aggregation. [claim:C045] [evidence:E025]
EQ-5D distinguishes a descriptive health profile, a person's own health rating and an index based on population preferences; the latter can support quality-adjusted life-year calculations.[@E025] These quantities answer different questions. [claim:C046] [evidence:E025]
A self-rating should not be substituted for a preference-weighted index, and a predicted QALY change requires defensible health trajectories over time. [claim:C047] [evidence:E025]
Our proposal retains such measures where appropriate while recording other consequences separately. [claim:C048] [evidence:E025]
It does not claim to replace them or to infer a QALY loss from a single night of lost sleep. [claim:C049] [evidence:E025]


## Why AI changes the comparison

Three distinctions organize the proposed AI application. [claim:C050] [evidence:E011]
First, task efficiency differs from control over the resulting time. [claim:C051] [evidence:E011]
Noy and Zhang's randomized study of professional writing found improved task productivity with ChatGPT.[@E011] Such evidence establishes a task-level benefit in a defined setting; it does not establish the destination of any saved time or a subsequent health gain. [claim:C052] [evidence:E011]
Verification, corrections, learning to use the tool and additional assignments must enter the accounting. [claim:C053] [evidence:E011]
The same technical gain could be followed by more rest, more production or a different activity mix, depending on constraints and preferences. [claim:C054] [evidence:E011]


For matched tasks with comparable quality, let T0 denote time without AI and T1 all time with AI, including prompting, checking and rework. [claim:C055] [evidence:E900]
Let S = T0 − T1, A denote time for additional tasks, and B other changes in work time within the same calendar window. [claim:C056] [evidence:E900]
Then: [claim:C057] [evidence:E900]


ΔW = −S + A + B.                                                     (1) [claim:C058] [evidence:E900]


Equation (1) is an accounting identity, not an estimated behavioural relationship. [claim:C059] [evidence:E900]
Under the additional assumption of a fixed total time budget, non-work time changes by −ΔW; its composition remains undetermined. [claim:C060] [evidence:E900]
In a hypothetical example, saving 240 minutes while adding 300 minutes of work, with B = 0, increases total work by 60 minutes. [claim:C061] [evidence:E900]
This example contains no health coefficient. [claim:C062] [evidence:E900]
Establishing a health pathway requires observing what activities change and the relevant outcomes, rather than converting minutes saved directly into health gains. [claim:C063] [evidence:E900]


Second, assisted performance and independent capability may diverge. [claim:C064] [evidence:E012]
A classroom experiment comparing different generative AI tutoring arrangements showed that the design of assistance matters for later unaided mathematics performance.[@E012] The outcome concerns learning in a particular educational setting, not general cognitive deterioration. [claim:C065] [evidence:E012]
The implication for assessment is to distinguish successful performance with assistance, retained ability without it, and the person's reasons for delegating a task. [claim:C066] [evidence:E012]
Delegation can be valuable even when independent mastery is not the objective. [claim:C067] [evidence:E012]


Third, immediate experience and lasting functioning answer different questions. [claim:C068] [evidence:E013,E014,E015]
In the longitudinal component of research on AI companions, interaction relieved loneliness immediately, but the reported pattern did not establish persistent improvement before subsequent interactions.[@E013] In medical information tasks, evaluating the user matters as much as evaluating the model: a randomized study found that ordinary information sources outperformed pooled AI-assisted users in identifying relevant conditions in hypothetical scenarios.[@E014] Neither finding supports a general conclusion that AI is beneficial or harmful. [claim:C069] [evidence:E013,E014]
Earlier research on Facebook deactivation likewise illustrates that different welfare-relevant outcomes can move in different directions, although that study is not evidence about generative AI.[@E015] [claim:C070] [evidence:E015]


These distinctions motivate a common comparison procedure across work, information use and leisure. [claim:C071] [evidence:E900]
They do not imply that all digital activities share an exposure or mechanism. [claim:C072] [evidence:E900]
Recommendation feeds, conversational companions, clinical prediction tools and writing assistants should be recorded separately, with their versions, uses and alternatives. [claim:C073] [evidence:E900]


Recent work on health-literate AI already emphasizes comprehension, agency, accountability and proportionality.[@E024] This closely related argument reinforces the need to define a narrower contribution. [claim:C074] [evidence:E024]
The proposed profile focuses on the economic comparison of feasible activities over time and on whether an additional comparison module improves decisions. [claim:C075] [evidence:E024]
Supporting understanding or invoking agency alone would not distinguish it from existing work. [claim:C076] [evidence:E024]


## A comparative health consequence profile

The proposed profile begins with a feasible decision, not a health score. [claim:C077] [evidence:E900]
For an individual i, an option a is compared with an available alternative b over horizon T. [claim:C078] [evidence:E900]
The record contains changes in disposable resources, the allocation of time, physical and mental functioning, experience, and relevant capabilities, together with constraints and the provenance of each prediction. [claim:C079] [evidence:E900]
Figure 1 summarizes these stages. [claim:C080] [evidence:E900]
Table 1 specifies a minimum record rather than an item bank that every study must administer. [claim:C081] [evidence:E900]


The first step is to make the counterfactual complete. [claim:C082] [evidence:E900]
A week of overtime followed by three weeks of ordinary activity cannot be compared with four weeks of overtime while ignoring the remaining weeks in the first option. [claim:C083] [evidence:E900]
Costs should be incremental and attributable to the option: total income is different from an overtime supplement, and a medical expenditure is different from a health loss. [claim:C084] [evidence:E900]
The profile should also identify effects on other people. [claim:C085] [evidence:E900]
An individual account and a household or societal account may require different perspectives; they should not be silently merged. [claim:C086] [evidence:E900]


The second step is to distinguish outcomes from their determinants. [claim:C087] [evidence:E016]
Sleep schedules, diet and sedentary time can be exposures or intermediate variables; symptoms, functioning and clinical outcomes belong to other parts of the causal account. [claim:C088] [evidence:E016]
Counting each stage as an independent welfare loss can duplicate the same consequence. [claim:C089] [evidence:E016]
WHO's ICF framework provides a useful distinction between functioning and its environmental context.[@E016] For this application, skin symptoms, vision, muscle function or metabolic measures should be included when they have a defined role in the decision, not because a larger testing panel appears more comprehensive. [claim:C090] [evidence:E016]
There is no universally favourable direction for every laboratory value. [claim:C091] [evidence:E016]


The third step is to match measures to their constructs and time scales. [claim:C092] [evidence:E017]
For example, WHO-5 measures mental well-being over the preceding two weeks; it is not a measure of total health value or a diagnostic conclusion.[@E017] Independent task performance, perceived autonomy and immediate enjoyment require their own interpretation. [claim:C093] [evidence:E017]
A person can value an activity's pleasure without claiming that it improves long-term health. [claim:C094] [evidence:E017]
Equally, a short-term functional change should not be described as permanent damage without evidence of persistence and mechanism. [claim:C095] [evidence:E017]


The fourth step is to make uncertainty visible. [claim:C096] [evidence:E900]
Each predicted consequence should specify its source population, comparison, outcome, horizon and uncertainty, and distinguish an observed finding from an extrapolation. [claim:C097] [evidence:E900]
Unknown is an admissible entry, distinct from zero. [claim:C098] [evidence:E900]
Where probabilities cannot be defended, transparent scenarios are preferable to invented precision. [claim:C099] [evidence:E900]
Recovery should be described by the outcome actually observed and the time needed to observe it; irreversibility is an empirical property to investigate, not a default label for every burden. [claim:C100] [evidence:E900]


Finally, appraisal belongs to the person within the stated normative and institutional boundaries. [claim:C101] [evidence:E009]
Dominance may resolve a comparison only when relevant dimensions are sufficiently complete, directions of preference are defensible and uncertainty does not overturn the ordering. [claim:C102] [evidence:E009]
Otherwise, the profile supports an explained trade-off or an explicitly unresolved choice. [claim:C103] [evidence:E009]
It does not force complete rankings. [claim:C104] [evidence:E009]
If aggregation is requested, the value functions, ranges and preference assumptions must be elicited and tested; weights cannot be justified merely because they sum to one.[@E009] Population policy decisions additionally require a stated distributional perspective rather than extrapolation from one person's willingness to accept a risk. [claim:C105] [evidence:E009]


## Applications and limits of inference

The overtime example shows why a decision can be important without having an immediately computable answer. [claim:C106] [evidence:E018]
A WHO/ILO systematic review reported a relative risk of stroke incidence of 1.35 (95% confidence interval 1.13–1.61) for working at least 55 rather than 35–40 hours per week in the selected cohort synthesis.[@E018] The evidence warrants attention to long working hours, but the estimate does not quantify an individual's risk from a particular month of late nights. [claim:C107] [evidence:E018]
Applying it to that problem would require a defensible exposure model, baseline risk and appropriate time horizon. [claim:C108] [evidence:E018]
Income can be compared under stated assumptions; overall welfare remains conditional on health consequences, alternatives and the person's goals. [claim:C109] [evidence:E018]


Suppose, purely illustratively, that an extra CNY 10,000 is offered for seven days of overtime and CNY 100,000 for 28 days. [claim:C110] [evidence:E900]
Assume that these are net supplements, ordinary earnings are otherwise equal, and the first option is followed by 21 days without overtime. [claim:C111] [evidence:E900]
Over the shared 28-day window, the second option adds CNY 90,000 and 21 overtime days. [claim:C112] [evidence:E900]
The financial increment is calculable under those assumptions; neither its health cost nor its welfare advantage is supplied by the arithmetic. [claim:C113] [evidence:E900]
The comparison should record daily exposure, recovery opportunities and later consequences, extending follow-up beyond the payment period when necessary. [claim:C114] [evidence:E900]
No amount is labelled adequate compensation without those additional judgements. [claim:C115] [evidence:E900]


Food choices expose a different limitation. [claim:C116] [evidence:E019,E020]
A controlled crossover trial found higher energy intake during an ultra-processed diet than an unprocessed diet, while a sleep-extension trial found reduced energy intake relative to control in adults with overweight and habitual short sleep.[@E019][@E020] These studies support attention to interactions among everyday activities. [claim:C117] [evidence:E019,E020]
They do not supply household food budgets, preparation costs or a universal ranking of people who choose differently. [claim:C118] [evidence:E019,E020]
Free meal provision, available time and actual purchasing conditions affect the relevance of feeding-study results to daily decisions. [claim:C119] [evidence:E019,E020]
Table 2 preserves the principal design and interpretation limits. [claim:C120] [evidence:E019,E020]


Digital companionship provides a third application. [claim:C121] [evidence:E013]
Immediate relief may matter even if it does not persist after an interaction.[@E013] The relevant alternative could be another form of entertainment, human contact, unwanted isolation or sleep; assuming that everyone is replacing a beneficial outdoor activity prejudges the comparison. [claim:C122] [evidence:E013]
A useful record would distinguish what the person gains during the activity, what happens afterwards, and which activities are displaced. [claim:C123] [evidence:E013]
Increasing or decreasing screen time is not by itself an adequate success criterion. [claim:C124] [evidence:E013]


Together, these examples show why health-related choices can be economically meaningful without a common currency. [claim:C125] [evidence:E900]
They also delimit the proposed contribution: a profile organizes the comparison; it does not generate missing medical knowledge or establish that people have overlooked consequences. [claim:C126] [evidence:E900]
That latter claim requires direct evidence of omission or misunderstanding in the target population. [claim:C127] [evidence:E900]


## Testing whether the framework improves decisions

The framework needs two distinct tests. [claim:C128] [evidence:E900]
A mechanism study would investigate how access to a specified AI system changes task time, total work, control over time, activity allocation and subsequent outcomes. [claim:C129] [evidence:E900]
Randomizing access could identify an assignment effect in that environment; it would not automatically randomize actual use, organizational practice or each mediator. [claim:C130] [evidence:E900]
A credible analysis must distinguish the total effect of assignment from additional assumptions required for mediation. [claim:C131] [evidence:E900]
Recording task quality and activities outside the software prevents platform telemetry from being mistaken for a complete account of life. [claim:C132] [evidence:E900]


A decision-support study addresses a different question: whether the proposed comparison procedure adds value. [claim:C133] [evidence:E010]
We propose an active-control comparison between an established guide with a common factual information pack and the same guide and facts supplemented by a separate module on time allocation, intertemporal consequences and uncertainty. [claim:C134] [evidence:E010]
The original Ottawa guide would remain intact.[@E010] This design tests the added package, including its burden. [claim:C135] [evidence:E010]
It cannot attribute an effect to each component; a component study would require a separate design. [claim:C136] [evidence:E010]


Decision quality should be distinguished from the process of deciding and from what happens afterwards.[@E021] The proposed primary outcome is independently scored understanding of the common information, including the comparator, horizon and limits of inference. [claim:C137] [evidence:E021]
An unfamiliar transfer task would assess whether any benefit extends beyond the presented layout. [claim:C138] [evidence:E021]
Confidence calibration, time required, non-completion and follow-up participation are important additional outcomes. [claim:C139] [evidence:E021]
A change towards a lower-paying job, less AI use or a preferred diet would not be scored as intrinsically correct. [claim:C140] [evidence:E021]
People may rationally revise their priorities after receiving information. [claim:C141] [evidence:E021]


Evidence from treatment and screening decision aids supports the feasibility of improving knowledge, but it does not determine the incremental effect of this module over an active comparator.[@E022] Sample size should therefore follow a justified target difference and a pilot assessment of outcome variability and retention, rather than importing a favourable pooled effect. [claim:C142] [evidence:E022]
Outcome collection should invite all randomized participants, including those who stop using the tool. [claim:C143] [evidence:E022]
Missing data, multiple outcomes, subgroup analyses and departures from the analysis plan should be addressed prospectively. [claim:C144] [evidence:E022]
Initial fixed materials can keep information constant; introducing a generative interface would create an additional intervention requiring separate evaluation. [claim:C145] [evidence:E022]


The framework should be revised if it adds burden without improving understanding, fails to transfer, obscures important trade-offs or systematically excludes people with fewer resources. [claim:C146] [evidence:E900]
A positive understanding result would support a decision aid, not establish a new theory of all health value. [claim:C147] [evidence:E900]
Conversely, a null tool effect would not show that health consequences are irrelevant to welfare. [claim:C148] [evidence:E900]
These distinctions make the programme capable of yielding informative negative findings. [claim:C149] [evidence:E900]


## Structural implications and boundaries

Individual decision support has a limited remit. [claim:C150] [evidence:E900]
Recording that someone cannot refuse overtime identifies a constraint; it does not remove it. [claim:C151] [evidence:E900]
Studying how employers, platforms, households and workers share gains and burdens requires evidence about those actors and the available institutional alternatives. [claim:C152] [evidence:E900]
The Marxian motivation is retained as a question about control and distribution, rather than treated as an empirical conclusion produced by the profile. [claim:C153] [evidence:E900]
Making health consequences more visible must not become a way of transferring responsibility for structural conditions to individuals. [claim:C154] [evidence:E900]


This Perspective is based on purposive reading across economic theory, decision support and selected health and AI studies. [claim:C155] [evidence:E900]
It is not a systematic review, and the examples do not estimate a common effect. [claim:C156] [evidence:E900]
Their populations, interventions and horizons differ. [claim:C157] [evidence:E900]
The proposed measures have not been validated together, preferences may change, and longer-term predictions can be weak even when short-term causal evidence is strong. [claim:C158] [evidence:E900]
Restricting the record to what can be measured may itself omit valued aspects of life; expanding it indefinitely can make it unusable. [claim:C159] [evidence:E900]
Both problems need participatory development and empirical assessment. [claim:C160] [evidence:E900]


Health economic evaluation in the AI era should make the relation between technical gains and lived consequences explicit. [claim:C161] [evidence:E900]
The proposed framework standardizes the questions, comparisons and evidence requirements while leaving room for different defensible choices. [claim:C162] [evidence:E900]
Its contribution will depend on whether it improves understanding and makes constraints visible at an acceptable burden. [claim:C163] [evidence:E900]
Productivity, enjoyment and health can each matter; the scientific task is to establish how they change under feasible alternatives, and the evaluative task is to explain how those changes bear on the lives people seek to lead. [claim:C164] [evidence:E900]
