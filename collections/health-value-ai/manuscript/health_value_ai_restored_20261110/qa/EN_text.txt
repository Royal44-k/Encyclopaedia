Health economic value in the AI era: a
framework for intertemporal choice
Richard T. Millian
Hangzhou Normal University
Abstract
Overtime pay, convenient meals and faster AI-assisted work offer visible gains in money or time. Changes in sleep,
physical burden and recovery may receive less attention. Drawing on health capital, time allocation, health capability
and decision analysis, this Perspective proposes a framework for comparing health-related consequences. It compares
feasible options over a common horizon, recording resources and time, health and functioning, experience, and
capabilities and agency before appraisal against personal priorities. Measurement, prediction and judgement remain
distinct; outcomes retain their units, timing and uncertainty. For AI use, faster tasks need not create more rest, and
better assisted performance need not improve independent ability. Cases involving daily and repeated overtime, food
choices and digital leisure show how to apply the comparison. Testable propositions concern the distribution of time
gains, persistence of effects and decision quality. The framework aims to clarify choices and their consequences while
allowing priorities to differ across people and circumstances.
Keywords: health economic value; intertemporal choice; artificial intelligence; health capability; time allocation; decision support

Whether overtime pay improves daily life depends on
both the income gained and what it requires. Longer
daytime work, repeated night shifts and greater
efficiency with AI may all increase income, with
different implications for sleep, energy and family time.
Comparison begins with concrete questions: how much
money is gained, where time goes, and whether the
person can work, study or care for family the next day.
Figure 1 brings these questions together; Figure 2
separates measurement, prediction and appraisal.
Established theories offer different starting points.
Grossman treats health as a stock with both consumption
and production value; Becker brings non-market time
into resource allocation1,2. Marx links the working day
to bargaining and the reproduction of labour power3.
These accounts address health, time and labour relations
from distinct perspectives. Marx motivates questions
about who receives production gains and who bears
health burdens. Identifying a particular health loss still
requires evidence about exposure and the feasible
alternative; it cannot simply be added to surplus value as
a measured quantity.
Health capability concerns both achieved health and the
real opportunities people have to maintain it and pursue
their goals4. A person who needs overtime pay for
housing or care may have the right to refuse without the
means to do so. Health-dependent utility also means that
the same income increment need not yield the same
welfare gain in every health state5. Appraisal should
therefore consider which difficulties the income relieves
and the time and health costs of obtaining it.
We use health economic value to describe the
significance of health and functioning for resource
allocation and personal choice, including the value of
health itself and its support for daily activities. It is
distinct from earning capacity, total welfare and human
worth. The framework specifies feasible alternatives,
traces their consequences over time, and makes
evidence, uncertainty and personal priorities explicit. A
comparison may yield a preference, identify the
conditions under which a choice is justified, or leave a
disagreement unresolved.

A laboratory report can describe a blood glucose level,
but cannot by itself establish whether a choice is
worthwhile. Measurement describes the current state;
intervention research estimates possible consequences of
changing diet; willingness to bear costs in money, time
or taste depends on personal priorities. Measurement,
prediction and appraisal therefore require different
justifications. Reference ranges do not rank human
worth, and average study results do not directly specify
an individual future.
Monetary valuation can inform defined policy and
resource-allocation questions, but payment does not
establish that lost functioning has been restored. Even
when a loss is irreversible, people still compare
prevention, mitigation and feasible alternatives.
Acceptance of compensation can reflect a considered
trade-off or a lack of other ways to meet essential needs.
Behavioural welfare analysis therefore examines the
conditions under which observed choices reveal
informed preferences6.
Cost–consequence analysis presents outcomes alongside
one another, while multicriteria decision analysis makes
criteria and weights explicit7,8. Established decision
guides already elicit options, values and support needs9.
Our proposal adds specific records of where saved time
goes, how outcomes develop, how functioning differs
with and without assistance, and who bears the
consequences. Table 1 organizes these fields. Their
usefulness must be tested against existing tools.
Standardized health-status instruments can supply
comparable information; preference-based measures
such as EQ-5D can support health-economic evaluation
when used with the appropriate valuation framework10.
They do not exhaust autonomy, relationships or
personally valued capability. Self-determination theory
offers a complementary account of autonomy,
competence and relatedness, without implying that every
activity has the same meaning for every person11.
Reading, gaming, exercise and companionship therefore
enter as alternatives whose consequences require
assessment, rather than predetermined categories of
virtue or harm.
A common record should accommodate different
priorities. When income, sleep and family time conflict,
the comparison should show the trade-off and test
whether changing priorities changes the choice. An
overall score is interpretable only when scales, weights,
uncertainty and double counting have been addressed.
Outcomes without a defensible conversion can retain
their own units and be reported separately.

Let xi(a;τ) denote person i's consequence profile under
feasible option a at time τ. Components retain their own
units: disposable resources, time allocation, health and
functioning, experience and capabilities. For
components with a meaningful numerical difference,
define:
 Δxi(a,b;τ) = xi(a;τ) − xi(b;τ). (1)
This is a contrast, not a causal prediction model.
Categorical changes remain descriptive. Every estimated
component requires a population, comparator, horizon,
source and uncertainty statement. Timing may require a
trajectory rather than an endpoint. Let zj denote an
appropriately oriented criterion, with larger values
preferred within its applicable range. Conditional
dominance requires:
 Δzj ≥ 0 (∀j); Δzk > 0 (∃k). (2)
This ordering applies only if the criteria cover the
relevant consequences and uncertainty does not overturn
it. Many biomarkers are not monotonic, and omitted
consequences can reverse an apparent ranking. Where
trade-offs remain, personal appraisal is necessary; no
universal exchange rate follows from equations (1)–(2).
For AI-assisted work, let S = T0 − T1 be time saved on a
fixed task; T1 includes prompting, verification and
rework. Let A be time added through additional tasks
and B all other net changes in work time, defined to
avoid overlap. Then:
 ΔW = −S + A + B. (3)
Figure 3 illustrates the identity. Saving four task hours
produces four fewer work hours if A = B = 0, one fewer
if A = 3 and B = 0, and one more if A = 5 and B = 0.
These are hypothetical allocations, not estimated AI
effects. Even a fall in total work does not determine
health: the activities replacing work, discretion over
them and their subsequent consequences remain to be
measured.

Task efficiency is the most direct outcome. Noy and
Zhang found faster writing and better output in a
randomized study of 453 professionals12. Brynjolfsson
and colleagues examined a staggered rollout covering
5,172 customer-support agents; their main
difference-in-differences estimate indicated about 15%
more issues resolved per hour13. The latter study was
quasi-experimental, measured throughput and lacked
compensation data. These studies support productivity
gains, but who controls the saved time, and whether
income or health improves, require separate
measurement13.
A second outcome is capability. Performance with
assistance and performance after assistance is removed
require separate measurement. Bastani and colleagues’
education experiment illustrates the need for an unaided
assessment of learning14. Evaluation should cover
immediate performance, independent performance and
transfer to unfamiliar tasks; this experiment does not
establish general cognitive harm from AI. Support itself
also has value when it makes previously difficult
activities accessible, even if unaided ability is
unchanged.
A third outcome is how long a benefit lasts. In De Freitas
and colleagues’ seven-day Study 3, loneliness relief
appeared mainly in immediate post-interaction
measures; persistent improvement before interaction was
not demonstrated15. Differential attrition and an
unmatched control duration limit interpretation.
Evaluation should record immediate comfort and follow
whether it lasts. For short-form content and virtual
interaction, relevant outcomes also include enjoyment,
sleep, relationships and the ability to stop. Activity
labels or duration alone do not establish their value.
Medical advice also raises the question of how
information becomes action. Bean and colleagues found
that users handling hypothetical medical scenarios did
not match the models’ standalone performance; Figure
5d shows identification in the control group relative to
pooled AI assistance16. This measures scenario
judgement, not clinical disease. Facebook-deactivation
research likewise examines life beyond platform use,
although its findings cannot directly establish generative
AI effects17. Health-literate AI emphasizes
comprehension, agency, accountability and
proportionality18. Separate measures of task
performance, sustained capability and daily outcomes
help put these requirements into practice.

Measures should follow the decision. A dinner
comparison may need price, preparation time and
satiety; a month of night work also calls for sleep,
daytime functioning and recovery measures. Establish
feasible alternatives and a common horizon, then select
measures relevant to baseline conditions and the
person’s concerns. Figure 4 distinguishes exposure, state
and consequences for daily life.
Separate exposures, states and consequences. Sleep
opportunity, sitting time, diet and screen use describe
behaviours or environments. Symptoms, laboratory
measures and task performance describe selected states.
Their significance depends on daily functioning,
experience and valued activities. The ICF helps situate
functioning in context19. Adding sedentary time, a
correlated biomarker and a downstream disease risk into
a single score can count the same pathway repeatedly.
Candidate causal links should therefore be specified
before aggregation or mediation analysis.
Glucose, lipids, blood pressure, strength, vision and skin
symptoms measure different aspects of health; “blood
quality” is too vague to replace them. Changes need
interpretation against baseline, measurement conditions
and appropriate ranges. Skin discomfort and appearance
can affect experience and social life, but complexion,
hair or body shape cannot establish internal organ status.
Relationships between physical and psychological health
also require measurement. Differences in health must not
become a ranking of dignity or human worth.
Experience and functioning should be observed at
appropriate times. WHO-5 records mental well-being
over the preceding two weeks; it is not a diagnostic test
or an instantaneous response measure20. A brief
attention task, an activity diary and a symptom report
likewise capture different constructs. Selection should
consider reliability, responsiveness, language,
accessibility and burden. Repeated measurement may
distinguish transient discomfort from a sustained
trajectory, but collecting more data is not automatically
better and can increase anxiety or exclusion.
Distinguish observations, predictions and unknowns. An
unmeasured effect is not zero; relative effects cannot
yield personal absolute risks without applicable baseline
risks and models. Keep personal priorities alongside the
evidence, and revisit the comparison when
circumstances or outcomes change.

Figure 5 preserves four different estimands. Long
working hours were associated with stroke incidence in a
cohort meta-analysis; this does not estimate the effect of
one overtime week21. Hall and colleagues' 20-person
inpatient crossover experiment found greater energy
intake on the ultra-processed diet, whereas Tasali and
colleagues' sleep-extension trial found lower estimated
energy intake relative to control22,23. The former
interval is one standard error, not a 95% confidence
interval. Bean and colleagues' odds ratio concerns
identification in medical scenarios, not the probability of
illness16.

checking the population, exposure, comparator, outcome
and follow-up. They show that particular consequences
of work, diet, sleep and digital tools can be measured.

a personal price for health. Figure 5 retains each study’s
units and uncertainty so that readers can judge its
relevance.

Case 1: daily pay and overnight supplements.
Suppose ordinary daily pay is CNY 200, with an
additional CNY 400 for working until 02:00 or CNY
1,000 for working through the night. Assuming all
amounts are net of tax and ordinary pay is unchanged,
daily income is CNY 200, 600 or 1,200 (Fig. 6). If CNY
400 and 1,000 instead denote total daily pay, the
increments are CNY 200 and 800. Distinguishing base
pay, supplements and total income prevents an initial
accounting error.
The schedule must then be specified: when work starts,
when the person must rise the next day, whether another
shift follows, and whether commuting or care duties can
change. Finishing at 02:00 with a day off is a different
option from finishing at 02:00 before an early shift. An
end time alone does not establish sleep duration, and
comparison should extend beyond the work episode to
subsequent functioning and recovery.
Whether the person gains also depends on what the
money enables. Paying rent or care costs may relieve an
immediate difficulty; fatigue, restricted activities and
other burdens still belong in the comparison.
Conversely, the absence of obvious discomfort does not
establish an absence of effects. Confirmed income
changes, functioning that needs observation and
unknown longer-term consequences can be listed
separately, allowing the person to identify losses they
regard as unacceptable.
Work arrangements may also be negotiable. Fewer
consecutive night shifts, time off the next day or the
ability to stop can be included as further options.
Comparison should examine whether they meet income
needs while preserving rest and valued activities. Their
effects still require evaluation.

Their activities, recovery opportunities and effects on

Appraisal also needs shift duration and timing, baseline
health, the ability to stop, essential financial needs and
the next-best feasible option. Different constraints may
lead to different choices, while health costs still belong
in the record. Whether CNY 100,000 is worthwhile can
only be judged under these conditions. A legal or
contractual payment addresses responsibility and
compensation; it does not show that lost functioning has
been restored.
Sleep restriction also shows why subjective adaptation
and objective function should be distinguished. In Van
Dongen and colleagues' laboratory programme, 35 adults
were randomized to 4, 6 or 8 hours in bed for 14 nights;
a separate total-deprivation experiment involved 13
adults. Performance deficits accumulated in the 4- and
6-hour groups, while reported sleepiness did not track
their full progression24. These functional findings do not
establish permanent neuronal destruction after one late
night. Recovery and persistence require their own
evidence.

Comparing a convenient purchased dinner with a meal
prepared at home requires more than ingredients or
price. Suppose the alternatives are maintained for four
weeks. Food expenditure, shopping, cooking and
cleaning time, taste and feasibility all enter the
comparison (Fig. 8). A person with a kitchen and
predictable hours faces different options from a shift

Junk food is an everyday label rather than a sufficiently
defined research exposure. Food composition,
processing category, intake and duration need
specification: takeaway is not synonymous with
ultra-processed food, nor home cooking with a balanced
diet. Hall and colleagues observed an energy-intake
difference, but no significant between-diet differences in
short-term glucose tolerance or continuous glucose
measures. This neither establishes long-term equivalence
nor supports deterioration in every internal measure22.
Preparing a meal uses time and labour. Who shops,
cooks and cleans should be recorded alongside food
spending and the time saved by convenience. A practical
comparison can begin with feasible changes to available
meal combinations or preparation burden, then observe
the eating experience and relevant outcomes. Requiring
an ideal menu at the outset can overlook the person’s
circumstances.

Suppose two hours after dinner are discretionary. Short
dramas or games, AI conversation, reading, walking and

Appraisal first asks what each displaces. Gaming may
displace intended sleep for one person and otherwise
unstimulating time alone for another. Online and offline
activities cannot be ranked by their labels alone, and
leisure need not justify every minute through increased
productivity.
Observation can extend from the evening to the next day
and subsequent weeks: was the activity enjoyable, did
satisfaction persist, could the person stop as planned, and
could they carry out valued activities the next day?
Attention needs a specified construct, such as sustained
attention, switching or memory. Patience likewise needs
a defined behaviour; viewing duration alone cannot
diagnose cognitive decline. These questions organize
observation rather than establish causal pathways.
AI companionship should be assessed for both
immediate support and sustained change. Studies have
observed loneliness relief after interaction, while
follow-up and comparator design limit conclusions about
persistence15. A personal comparison can track whether
use adds to existing support, accompanies delayed sleep
and can be ended as intended. Enjoyment and support
have value in themselves; observing subsequent changes
can inform decisions about future use.
The same principle applies to AI food or medical advice.
A credible-sounding answer and an appropriate user
decision are different outcomes; medical-scenario
experiments have demonstrated a gap between models
acting alone and human–AI interactions16. Evaluation
should examine understanding, recognition of
applicability and appropriate action, rather than treating
verbal fluency as effectiveness.

Validation has two parts: identifying mechanisms that
change health-related outcomes, and testing whether the
comparison framework improves informed choice. The
first supplies evidence for the record; the second tests the
tool’s usefulness. Figure 10 separates these research
paths, and Table 3 lists propositions and findings that
could weaken them.
Workplace studies could combine task logs, whole-day
diaries and repeated outcomes with measures of control
over workload and schedules. Randomized policies or
credible natural experiments would strengthen causal
inference. Simple user–non-user comparisons remain
vulnerable to job selection, workload, prior health and
reverse causation. Prespecified mechanism analyses
should avoid obscuring total effects through mechanical
adjustment for post-treatment time use.
Tool evaluation should use an active comparator:
identical evidence and an established guide, with only
the intervention group receiving the added record of
time, consequences and uncertainty. Decision-quality
research and the Cochrane review inform comparator
and outcome selection25,26. Prespecify understanding as
a potential primary outcome with independent scoring;
transfer, values–choice concordance and calibrated
uncertainty can be secondary outcomes.
Record completion time, attrition, anxiety, accessibility
and withdrawals’ experiences. Repeated use may bring
learning, fatigue or changed decisions. Preregister the
population, target effect, sample-size basis, missing-data
analysis and multiplicity handling. Interviews can
explain understanding or disagreement, but cannot
replace causal estimates. Simplify or discontinue a
module without meaningful net benefit. Choices closer
to the researchers’ preferred lifestyle alone do not
establish success.

Work rules, platform design, housing, food access and
care responsibilities constrain personal choices (Fig. 11).
Income insecurity or workload demands may make rest
difficult, so an emphasis on self-discipline alone misses
the conditions limiting choice. The framework can
reveal these constraints; expanding feasible options and
changing bargaining conditions also require
organizational and public-policy action.
The perspective of the comparison must also be explicit.
Workers’ net income, firms’ output, unpaid family care
and public spending are separate entries. Compensation
is income to a recipient but may be a transfer between
parties at the societal level. Time saved by one person
may become another’s checking, correction or care
work. Comparing individual, household and societal
outcomes shows both aggregate change and who bears it.
Institutions can also use the framework to ask concrete
questions: does a productivity policy return time to
workers; does a platform permit meaningful
disengagement; and does an intervention remain feasible
for people with limited money, mobility or digital
access? These are evaluative questions, not established
effects of particular policies. The same information
should not become a surveillance score used to penalize
workers, deny opportunities or blame people for
constrained choices. Purpose limitation and
proportionate collection are part of making the proposed
support usable.
This Perspective brings health-related consequences,
their timing and their distribution into one comparison.
AI use makes these questions pressing because faster
task performance need not improve independent
capability or daily life. A shared record can make such
differences explicit while allowing personal priorities to
vary. Whether the framework improves explanation,
prediction or decision quality beyond existing methods
remains an empirical question.
This conceptual Perspective draws together economic
theory, measurement approaches and selected empirical
studies through narrative synthesis. The proposed
framework remains unvalidated. Broader measurement
may add burden, population effects may not apply to
individuals, and priorities can change with
circumstances. Context-specific feasibility studies
should precede comparison with established decision
support, with follow-up to assess whether gains in
understanding and choice persist.
